# Procedural city and town generation: street networks, parcels, building massing (for a 32³-chunk voxel engine)

Scope note: the engine stores worlds as 32-cubed voxel chunks and would generate a city whole at world creation, so global (non-streaming) algorithms are acceptable. Target games: a dense Shadows-of-Doubt-style city of multi-storey buildings, and small-map town building. Sources were gathered 2026-11-11; papers are older, tool/game facts were checked against 2024–2026 pages where possible. Where a fact comes from a secondary summary rather than the primary paper this is flagged.

## Key question 1: Road / street network generation (L-systems, tensor fields, grids, organic layouts, space colonisation, Citygen, indie approaches, hierarchy)

### Takeaway
Three families dominate: (a) Parish & Müller's "extended L-system" which in practice is a priority-queue segment grower with `globalGoals` (population map + superimposed pattern rule) and `localConstraints` (snap-to-intersection, prune/rotate at obstacles), (b) tensor-field streamline tracing (Chen et al. 2008; ProbableTrain's open TypeScript port) which gives smooth, hierarchical grid/radial networks almost for free, and (c) polygon-partition "organic" layouts (Watabou: Voronoi/largest-inscribed-rectangle cuts). For a voxel grid city the Parish & Müller grower with the "New York" rule, or a tensor field with grid basis fields, maps most cleanly onto axis-aligned blocks; tensor fields are the better choice if the engine wants mixed radial/grid downtowns and coast-following streets.

### Cited Findings

**Parish & Müller 2001, "Procedural Modeling of Cities" (SIGGRAPH) — primary text read from the ETH PDF**
- Inputs are image maps: elevation, population density, zone maps (residential/commercial/mixed) and "street patterns (control behavior of streets)"; example figure describes "an imaginary virtual city of 20 miles diameter" — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Two road classes: "Highways connect areas with highly concentrated population globally, by scanning the population density input map for peaks. Streets cover the areas between highways according to local population density." — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Highway global goal: the road end "shoots radial rays" within a preset radius, samples population along each ray (weighted by distance), and turns toward the best peak. Streets do not follow the population gradient; they follow the superimposed pattern and "When an area with no population is reached, the streets stop growing." — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Pattern rules (blendable over the map via a greyscale control map): Basic (no superimposed pattern, "natural growth"), New York/checkers ("follows a given global or local angle and maximal length and width of a single block"), Paris/radial ("highways follow radial tracks around a center"), San Francisco ("follow the route of the least elevation. Roads on different height levels are connected by smaller streets, that follow steepest elevation and are short") — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- `localConstraints` does two checks: (1) does the segment end inside/cross an illegal area (water, park) → prune length "up to a certain factor", rotate "within a maximal angle", or for highways allow crossing illegal area "up to a specified length" and flag the segment for a bridge/tunnel at geometry time; (2) search for intersections or existing roads/crossings "within a specified distance to the segment end" → three events: two streets intersect → crossing; ends close to an existing crossing → extend to reach it; close to intersecting → extend to form intersection. "After a crossing is generated, a road stops growing." — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Loops are explicitly allowed ("self-sensitive L-system"); topology becomes net-like rather than tree-like. Straight segments are smoothed afterwards with a subdivision scheme. The Manhattan example shows the network "after 28 and 142 steps" of the parallel rewriting. — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Module attributes carried per segment: `del` (delay), `ruleAttr[0-2]`, `roadAttr[0-2]` (length, angle, …), and a `state` ∈ {UNASSIGNED, SUCCEED, FAILED}; the L-system rules p1–p9 just sequence calls to external `globalGoals`/`localConstraints` functions — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Practical reimplementation (phiresky, following Sean Barrett 2008): replace the L-system with a priority queue keyed on delay: dequeue min, `applyLocalConstraints` (quadtree lookup), if accepted push `globalGoalsGenerate(segment)`; stop at `SEG_LIMIT`. Highways are longer and branch less; normal streets branching off highways get an extra delay so highways are not cut off; branch "with some probability, at roughly 90 degrees"; demo note "10000 segments, not full speed". Population map used: `((simplex(x/10,y/10)*simplex(x/20+.5,y/20+.5) + simplex(x/20+1,y/20+1))/2)^2` (each term normalised to 0..1). No numeric branch probabilities given. — [phiresky Procedural Cities presentation](https://phiresky.github.io/procedural-cities/presentation.html)
- CityEngine's current "Grow Streets" tool (the commercial descendant) "builds major streets until they enclose blocks, then subdivides those blocks with minor streets" and routes around obstacles — [CityEngine docs: Generate street networks](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/cityengine/latest/help/help-grow-a-street.htm)

**Chen, Esch, Wonka, Müller, Zhang 2008, "Interactive Procedural Street Modeling" (tensor fields)**
- User designs a tensor field (from water/park maps plus brush strokes, smoothing, constraints, noise, rotation fields); streets are generated from it, then 3D geometry. Authors state "a city with reasonable complexity can be modeled within five minutes, but required an additional thirty to sixty minutes to fine tune the details." C++ source (VS2005-era, unchanged since publication, no stated licence) is downloadable from the project page. — [Chen et al. project page](https://www2.cs.uh.edu/~chengu/Publications/streetModeling/street_modeling.html); paper PDF: [street_sig08.pdf](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~zhange/images/street_sig08.pdf)
- ProbableTrain's open-source reimplementation (TypeScript, "LGPL-3.0" per README, GPL-3.0 per repo metadata, ~1.3k stars): each 2D point holds a tensor with major/minor eigenvectors; the field is a smooth blend of **grid** and **radial** basis fields; roads are streamlines integrated with **RK4** at step `dstep` along major or minor direction, stopping on leaving the domain, iteration limit, or looping; `dsep` is the minimum streamline spacing enforced by a grid-storage structure and is the density knob; three road classes main/major/minor are traced with different `dsep`. Water: one streamline traced through a simplex-noise-perturbed field becomes coastline/river; tensors inside water are zero so streamlines stop. Parks: polygons found in the major graph before minor roads. Graph: streamlines simplified (simplify-js), intersected (isect), nodes with neighbours; blocks found by walking "turning right at each intersection" (assumes no dead ends — stated limitation). Lots: block polygon shrunk then "repeatedly split along their longest edge". Runs single-threaded; animated mode budgets ~30 ms per frame. — [MapGenerator algorithm overview (raw docs)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProbableTrain/MapGenerator/master/docs/algorithmoverview.md); [GitHub ProbableTrain/MapGenerator](https://github.com/ProbableTrain/MapGenerator); [itch page](https://probabletrain.itch.io/city-generator)

**Organic / medieval: Watabou's Medieval Fantasy City Generator (MFCG)**
- Early cities "were barely more than stylized Voronoi diagrams"; in 0.7.3 the author replaced a naive Bowyer–Watson with a Delaunator port for Voronoi construction ("much faster but was not a bottleneck") — [MFCG devlog 0.7.3](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/112574/073-bug-fixes-geojson-export)
- Blocks reuse the castle method: "take an original polygon, then try to find the largest rectangle which fits inside the polygon. After that if the rectangle is large enough I cut it into smaller blocks and throw away some of them." Walls/coast are random; "2 out of 3 layouts are coastal". — [MFCG devlog 0.3.0 and comments](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/3091/030-wall-less-layouts-city-outskirts-smooth-roads)
- Ward semantics (Gate, Craftsmen, Slum, Administration…): labels mark dominant venue type, not exclusive content; craftsmen live above workshops; the author called the original alley/building algorithm "a rather silly algorithm" producing too many triangular lots — [MFCG devlog "Some answers and comments"](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/1579/some-answers-and-comments)
- 0.5.3: neighbourhoods = clusters of adjacent same-type wards; alleys reworked to avoid triangular lots; building modes Off/Simple (rectangular where possible)/Complex; each district has layout parameters "such as how regular its alley grid is and how large its buildings are", with a "Continuous" option that interpolates parameters across neighbouring districts to avoid "tiny buildings facing huge ones across a street" — [MFCG devlog 0.5.3](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/34104/053-neighbourhoods-alleys-and-buildings)
- 0.7.0: districts "grown more organically" around prominent features with landscape-shaped borders, replacing neighbourhoods — [MFCG devlog 0.7.0](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/85275/070-districts)
- Source of the older generator: TownGeneratorOS, Haxe/OpenFL, **GPL-3.0**, "lacks some of the latest features, namely waterbodies, options UI" — [GitHub watabou/TownGeneratorOS](https://github.com/watabou/TownGeneratorOS)

**Space colonisation, Citygen, agent/other**
- A 2020-era method adapts space colonisation (attraction points from plant growth) to lay out roads in urban and inter-city settings as an alternative to L-systems — [search result summary citing the RCAAP record](https://rc.cplp.org/Record/rcaap_9e54a0998bc88a6c4dc32bc2b2ddd390/Description); an arXiv framework grows roads iteratively as population points appear, with a parameter α balancing on-road vs off-road travel speed — [arXiv 2001.08180](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.08180.pdf)
- Citygen (Kelly & McCabe, SIGGRAPH 2007 poster): user draws primary roads, system generates secondary roads and blocks inside enclosed regions; C++/OGRE/wxWidgets. Later work describes its secondary-road step as a graph-growing algorithm parameterised by "street length and its deviation range, minimum street length" (second-hand) — [SIGGRAPH history: Kelly & McCabe](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=142350); [Khronos COLLADA Citygen page](https://wikis.khronos.org/collada/Citygen); [Teng 2017 PCG workshop paper](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/teng2017semntic.pdf)
- Introversion's Subversion (cancelled): Chris Delay "started out with road layouts, and then began to modify the parameters"; settings produce radial/spiral or rigid grid, "American block logic or medieval European sprawl", and combining them is more believable; each subsequent layer examines spaces between the previous layers and decides skyscraper vs house; "Every subsequent layer builds on the previous layer" — [BLDGBLOG 2009](https://bldgblog.com/2009/08/procedural-destruction-and-the-algorithmic-fiction-of-the-city/)
- Indie 2D example (Vagabond): roads obtained by A* from each door to each door with a discount on already-used road segments; building neighbours found via Delaunay triangulation — [pvigier Vagabond city generation](https://pvigier.github.io/2020/03/15/vagabond-city-generation.html)
- Minecraft Lost Cities mod (MIT): 1.21.11-9.5.0 "added versioned hierarchical street generation, with primary, secondary, and tertiary roads, wider primary roads, slopes, connectors, and deterministic large bridges"; a later release adds "a versioned inter-city highway network which creates deterministic routes between likely city regions" — [Lost Cities on Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/the-lost-cities); [changelog](https://modrinth.com/mod/the-lost-cities/changelog)

### Inferences
- For a voxel engine, treat the Parish & Müller grower as "priority-queue segment growth with snapping" rather than as an L-system; the quadtree + 3 snapping events are the entire local-constraint machinery. Quantising segment endpoints to the voxel grid (and restricting the New York rule to 0°/90°) yields axis-aligned blocks directly.
- Tensor fields are attractive because block polygons fall out of the right-turn walk and the `dsep` per road class gives a street hierarchy (main/major/minor) with one parameter each; the engine must handle dead ends (ProbableTrain's limitation) or prune them before polygon finding.
- Watabou's approach (polygon partition + largest-inscribed-rectangle cuts) is the cheapest route to small medieval towns on small maps and already produces lots; it is GPL, so port the idea, not the code.

### Gaps
- Parish & Müller give no numeric branch probabilities, snap radii, or runtimes in the text I read; secondary reimplementations also omit them.
- I could not fetch the Chen et al. 2008 PDF (too large) so basis-field formulas (decay radius, field combination) are reported only via the ProbableTrain port.
- No primary write-up by Watabou on how wards are assigned to Voronoi patches was found; only devlog fragments.
- No usable source on Red Blob Games/Amit Patel town or road generation notes was found (search returned only hex/A* pages).

## Key question 2: Parcelling blocks into lots (OBB recursive split, straight skeleton / offset subdivision, Voronoi, street adjacency)

### Takeaway
Vanegas et al. 2012 define the two standard methods: recursive **OBB** splitting (split the oriented bounding box along its longest/widest edge until parcel width/area falls below a threshold; most regular parcels, maximises street-parallel orientation, works best on grids) and **straight-skeleton / offset** subdivision (offset the block inward by a depth, split the ring at a target parcel width perpendicular to the street, works best on curved loop/lollipop blocks). Both delete or merge parcels without street access; Parish & Müller's original "split longest approximately-parallel edges until under area threshold, discard lots without street access" is the simplest version and is what most open-source generators actually implement.

### Cited Findings
- Parish & Müller: blocks are "divided into smaller units using a simple, recursive algorithm that divides the longest edges that are approximately parallel until the subdivided lots are under a threshold area specified by the user"; concave allotments are forbidden; "all allotments that are too small or do not have direct access to a street are discarded"; blocks are first "created by scaling from street crossings" (inset) — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Vanegas, Kelly, Weber, Halatsch, Aliaga, Müller, "Procedural Generation of Parcels in Urban Modeling", CGF 31(2) 2012 pp. 681–690, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03047.x — [Purdue listing](https://cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/publications/vanegas2012procedural); [EG digital library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v31i2pp681-690)
- OBB (as summarised by the CIRRELT 2015 comparison): "recursively splitting street blocks into parcels. Use of the OBB produces more regularly shaped parcels, and ensures a maximum number are oriented parallel to an adjacent street. Their algorithm also tries to ensure street access by splitting the bounding box along either the longest or widest edge"; the width parameter "determines the width below which no more subdivision occurs" (a constraint, not a deterministic parcel width) — [CIRRELT-2015-26](https://www.cirrelt.ca/DocumentsTravail/CIRRELT-2015-26.pdf)
- Straight Skeleton (same summary): skeleton "formed by collapsing the edges of a polygon inward and tracing the intersection"; "This shape is then split at regular intervals determined by a user-specified parcel width. Diagonal edges are then shifted to be perpendicular to roads." Offset Subdivision adds "an additional parameter specifying the distance to set the far edge of the parcel" (the offset depth). Both use "block-level descriptive parcel parameters specified by the user, such as minimum parcel area and width"; slivers are merged into non-sliver parcels — [CIRRELT-2015-26](https://www.cirrelt.ca/DocumentsTravail/CIRRELT-2015-26.pdf)
- Comparison on real blocks (via ESRI CityEngine's implementation): "the Oriented Bounding Box algorithm performs better for warped grids, as well as gridiron and fragmented uniform sites. It also produces more similar parcel areas and widths … The Straight Skeleton algorithm performs better for loop and lollipop networks and also produces more similar parcel shapes." A disadvantage of the Parish-style method: "parcels with no street access are deleted, leaving holes in the middle of blocks" — [CIRRELT-2015-26](https://www.cirrelt.ca/DocumentsTravail/CIRRELT-2015-26.pdf)
- Tom Kelly's thesis extends the (weighted) straight skeleton; "the first application of the skeleton is to the subdivision of city blocks into parcels"; Aichholzer's motorcycle-graph algorithm is used to trace it — [Kelly thesis, Glasgow](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/4975)
- Galin et al. 2012 (villages) generate parcels by anisotropic "conquest" from building seeds: cost rises with distance from the seed (`d(p,n,t)=|max(p·n, p·t)|` for houses/villas), with water/wall/road costs and a slope cost so parcels elongate along terrain; "seeds that lose access to roads are suppressed" — [Galin et al. 2012 villages PDF](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2012-villages.pdf)
- ProbableTrain: lots are block polygon "shrunk, then repeatedly split along their longest edge", described as under development — [MapGenerator docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProbableTrain/MapGenerator/master/docs/algorithmoverview.md)
- Watabou MFCG: lots come from largest-inscribed-rectangle cuts of each polygon; 0.5.3 tuned alleys to avoid triangular lots and fits buildings tighter; a later "tight" option exists — [MFCG 0.3.0 comments](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/3091/030-wall-less-layouts-city-outskirts-smooth-roads); [MFCG 0.5.3](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/34104/053-neighbourhoods-alleys-and-buildings)

### Inferences
- On a voxel grid with axis-aligned blocks, OBB splitting degenerates to splitting rectangles along the long side until width < W_min and area < A_max, with an inset "block margin" for sidewalks; this is trivially voxel-exact and keeps every lot street-adjacent if splits are always perpendicular to the street edge. Straight-skeleton is only needed for non-rectangular (tensor-field or organic) blocks.
- The CIRRELT finding (OBB best for gridiron) means a Shadows-of-Doubt-style grid city needs only OBB; a medieval town with curved streets benefits from the offset/skeleton method or Watabou's rectangle-cut trick.

### Gaps
- The Vanegas 2012 PDF itself could not be fetched (Purdue copy >10 MB, EG library 403); exact parameter names (e.g. irregularity, split-edge choice, area/aspect thresholds) and runtime figures come only from the CIRRELT summary.

## Key question 3: Building massing, exteriors, zoning, and mapping shells onto a voxel grid

### Takeaway
Massing in the literature is either L-system extrusion of the lot footprint (Parish & Müller) or CGA shape grammar split/repeat/component-split rules (Müller 2006 → CityEngine), with height controlled by a zoning/land-value map. Voxel games converge on a different pattern: a coarse "tile" grid much larger than a voxel (Shadows of Doubt uses 1.8 m tiles, floors 15×15 tiles; Lost Cities uses one Minecraft chunk per building cell) and hand-authored floorplan presets stitched procedurally, with a one-cell perimeter wall and fixed floor height. No source gives a complete "shell-to-voxel" recipe; the engine will have to define it.

### Cited Findings
- Parish & Müller buildings: "For every allotment one building is generated"; three types (skyscrapers, commercial, residential) chosen by zoning image maps; L-system modules are transforms (scale/move), extrusion, branching, termination, plus geometric templates for roofs/antennae; the axiom is the building's bounding box so each derivation step is an LOD; max height is controlled "through the usage of an image map" to restrict skyscrapers to zones — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Facade texturing there uses "layered grids": one-dimensional interval groups (e.g. `Grid(3).scale(0.95)`) combined into 2D layers by eval functions (AND/OR), stacked so inactive points fall through to the layer below; per-cell random window variants — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Müller, Wonka, Haegler, Ulmer, Van Gool 2006 "Procedural Modeling of Buildings" (ACM TOG 25(3) 614–623) introduce CGA shape: the full split definition "including the repeat split and the scaling of rules", plus a component split onto faces/edges; replaced shapes are "marked as inactive" so the hierarchy can be queried; integrated into CityEngine — [SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=113547); [paper PDF (course copy)](https://course.ccs.neu.edu/cs5150f13/readings/muller_buildings.pdf)
- A gamedev tutorial example of CGA-style facades: split facade into first floor and upper floors, then "repeat split … into floors 1 unit high" — [gamedev.net shape grammar article](https://gamedev.net/tutorials/programming/engines-and-middleware/procedural-modeling-of-buildings-with-shape-grammars-r4596)
- Epic's UEFN procedural building template "stores a 3D voxel grid and runs a second pass that spawns meshes wherever the voxels change", with a rule that slices a tall box into one-voxel-high floor pieces and routes corners/walls to separate rules; it pairs shape grammar for buildings with WFC for flat areas — [UEFN procedural building template](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/uefn/procedural-building-template-in-unreal-editor-for-fortnite)
- Minecraft organic building generation (FDG 2019): generator split into floor-plan generation then external wall generation — [arXiv 1906.05094](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1906.05094); "In Ruins" castle generator marks interior cells as room tiles and perimeter cells as wall tiles, spacing rooms so walls do not double up — [nullpointer castle generation](https://www.nullpointer.co.uk/castle-generation-in-ruins.html)
- Shadows of Doubt tile system (see Q4): tiles 1.8 m × 1.8 m, floors 15 × 15 tiles, hierarchy City > District > Block > Building > Floor > Address > Room > Tile — [ColePowered DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-13-creating-procedural-interiors/)
- Townscaper: tiles are "corner segments rather than complete building chunks" placed Marching-Cubes-style on cell corners; modules carry priorities (garden highest, only in fully wall-enclosed flat areas); windows are cut with a stencil buffer not modelled in tiles; "A reskin would need roughly 500 tiles" — [Game Developer: How Townscaper Works](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Zoning / density gradients: Parish & Müller derive type and height from zone and population image maps (above); Watabou interpolates per-district "building size / alley regularity" parameters across borders ("Continuous") — [MFCG 0.5.3](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/34104/053-neighbourhoods-alleys-and-buildings); Shadows of Doubt's DensityMod exposes "density" and a "land value range" as the generator's knobs — [DensityMod on Thunderstore](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/Nova/DensityMod/); Margin City shows wealth follows district ("Heights homes have better furniture, finishes, and less grime") — [Margin City](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/The_Protectorate/MarginCity/)
- Watabou roofs: moved to a method "based on the idea of straight skeleton" — [MFCG 0.8.0](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/179534/080-new-buildings-improved-ui)

### Inferences
- A voxel-native massing pipeline consistent with the sources: lot rectangle (voxels) → setback inset → footprint → choose floor count from zone/land-value → stack floors of fixed height H voxels (SoD uses 1.8 m horizontal tiles; a voxel engine at 1 m would use 2-voxel-wide walls' worth of tile, or a 3–4-voxel floor height) → CGA-style split of each facade into bays of fixed voxel width for windows/doors → roof template. Floor height and tile size should be integer multiples of the voxel so floors align across adjacent buildings and streets.
- Because 32³ chunks are the storage unit, choosing a city tile = 32 voxels (one chunk column per lot slot, like Lost Cities' one-chunk cells) makes block layout, chunk generation and LOD coincide; Shadows of Doubt's 15-tile floors (27 m) are of the same order.

### Gaps
- No source states explicit wall-thickness or floor-height-in-voxels conventions for a 1 m voxel city; Shadows of Doubt's vertical floor height in metres was not found.
- The Müller 2006 PDF was not read in full; only the split/repeat/component-split summary is sourced.

## Key question 4: Shadows of Doubt — everything public about its city generation

### Takeaway
Shadows of Doubt (ColePowered, Unity, 1.0 released 2025) generates a grid city from a seed, size (4 tiers, ~550–730 citizens at the largest) and density/land-value settings; buildings are assembled from hand-designed floorplans (15×15 tiles of 1.8 m) whose addresses are then auto-partitioned into rooms by a scored placement search, so the "voxel" look is really a coarse tile grid with authored presets. City generation takes minutes and is saved as a large city file; a daily 10–15 s routine-planning pass drives NPC schedules.

### Cited Findings
- Hierarchy: "City > District > Block > Building > Floor > Address > Room > Tile"; "Each tile is 1.8m x 1.8m in-game" and the grid "supports pathfinding, culling, and generation"; "Building floors are 15 x 15 tiles" — [DevBlog 13: Creating Procedural Interiors](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-13-creating-procedural-interiors/)
- Floor editor: designers define address footprints on a floor; "Several floorplans can be combined to generate a building"; a "generate interior" button creates dividing walls; apartments always get a bathroom, larger ones can get bedrooms/kitchen/study/shower/dining; hallways are drawn when the address entrance is too far from a corner so rooms are not reachable only through other rooms — [DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-13-creating-procedural-interiors/)
- Room placement: rooms processed in importance order (living room, then bathroom…); every free-space placement is simulated and scored on (1) floor space, (2) uniform shape by corner count, (3) windows; highest-ranked placement is taken; repeat until no rooms/space. Rules: connection limits (every room may connect to a hallway; kitchen may connect to living room), door limits (bathroom one door), entrance restrictions (bedrooms cannot hold the address entrance), and an override system letting a room "steal" excess floorspace from an already-placed room — [DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-13-creating-procedural-interiors/)
- Early (2018) city layout: "procedurally generated using a semi-but-not-overly complex system involving building density and zoning", with a designed/editor city considered for later — [DevBlog 3](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-3-moving-forward/); an early devlog said floors were planned on a 5×5 grid with SimCity-like room zoning (secondary summary of DevBlog 5) — [DevBlog 5: Pivot!](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-5-pivot/)
- Simulation: "hundreds of simulated citizens", each making "roughly 4 to 10 journeys a day"; routines are planned before each day in "typically 10 to 15 seconds depending on population", with real-time deviations; sightings computed by a global loop over travelling citizens; citizens "can see into lit windows in the building across the street" — [DevBlog 8: Simulating a City](https://colepowered.com/?p=34142)
- Mix of authored and generated: "a unique mix of procedural generation and hand-crafted design enables every room of every building to be explored" — [DevBlog 28](https://colepowered.com/?p=35253)
- Per Wikipedia, the city layout, citizens, routines and all cases are procedurally generated per save — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadows_of_Doubt)
- Size tiers: a Steam post says "CitySize is a single digit number from 0 to 3" mapping to small/medium/large/very large; players report largest cities of "727 people" and typical "550-650"; PC Gamer: "a few buildings to a sprawling block filled with 600-or-so people" — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/986130/discussions/0/6274121610031111262); [PC Gamer review](https://pcgamer.com/games/sim/shadows-of-doubt-review)
- Generation time: "wait a few minutes for your brand new city to simulate" — [TechRaptor preview](https://techraptor.net/gaming/previews/shadows-of-doubt-preview-city-at-your-fingertips); larger sizes "take a hot minute to load when first generating" — [Overclockers blog](https://www.overclockers.co.uk/blog/dont-sleep-on-shadows-of-doubt/). No per-tier minute counts found.
- Generated cities are large files; loading a save without the city file regenerates it from the seed (secondary summary of a Steam thread) — [Steam: where are city generations saved](https://steamcommunity.com/app/986130/discussions/0/3844430784760412104/)
- City editor (official Notion): cities are seeded; editor is phased, phase 1 = placing buildings and renaming buildings/streets — [In-Game City Editor (Notion)](https://shadowsofdoubt.notion.site/In-Game-City-Editor-9ac9c55aedec4a4388234e4ae0488b8b); a 2024 user says editor layouts are "blocky" and the city "is capped at 4x5 slots" — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/986130/discussions/0/4518884696784394433)
- Mod evidence of the data model: CityPlanner lets you "paint which gaps between tiles are main roads, back streets, or alleys" — [CityPlanner changelog](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/The_Protectorate/CityPlanner/changelog); Margin City is "7 × 8 blocks inside its waterfront", ~1,000 residents, "larger than the game's biggest city size", and the plan supplies "districts, wealth, a building on every block and which way each one faces, and it still generates the streets, interiors and residents itself"; district list: The Heights, Park Terrace, Downtown, The Commons (park belt), Chinatown, Red Light, The Bricks, The Stacks — [Margin City](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/The_Protectorate/MarginCity/)
- DensityMod exposes "density" and "land value range" on the City Generation screen; warns higher density → long load times — [DensityMod](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/Nova/DensityMod/)
- Edge (Feb 2021) listing: new game lets you "generate your own rainy cyber-noir city, with size and population density sliders" — [PressReader Edge](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20201231/283940295295493)
- Procedural generation forbids shadow baking (secondary summary of DevBlog 12) — [DevBlog 12: HDRP](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-12-hdrp-makes-things-prettier/)

### Inferences
- The city model is: a small grid of block "slots" (mods: 4×5 cap in editor; 7×8 in a modded city), each slot holding one building with a facing, streets in the gaps between slots typed as main road/back street/alley, districts and a wealth/land-value field driving which floorplans and furnishing presets get picked. Street network generation is therefore trivial (grid gaps); the work is in the per-building floorplan assembly and room solver.
- "Voxel" in press coverage refers to the blocky 1.8 m tile aesthetic, not a true voxel storage model; for Strata the equivalent is tile = N voxels with authored presets expressed in tiles.

### Gaps
- No GDC/other talk by Cole Jefferies on city generation was found; the Wireframe/whynowgaming interview and the gameplay.tips building type guide were unreachable (redirect 403, 404), so the official building-type list (floors per type, basements, rooftops) is unsourced here.
- Per-size block counts and generation times are not published; the 4×5 slot cap is a user report.

## Key question 5: Other shipped examples (Townscaper, Minecraft villages/Lost Cities, Dwarf Fortress, Subversion, Cities: Skylines, Rust)

### Takeaway
Townscaper is the reference for WFC-on-an-irregular-grid (hex → random triangle pairing → ortho subdivision → relax); Minecraft villages are jigsaw template graphs with pools/weights/depth; Lost Cities is the most complete MIT voxel-city reference with chunk-cell buildings and, since 2025, a hierarchical street/highway system; Subversion was layered parameterised road/lot generation. Dwarf Fortress and Rust details were not well sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Townscaper grid (Sylves reconstruction): triangle grid (side 0.5, hex bounded 4 triangles per side) → `RandomPairing()` merges random triangle pairs into quads → Conway `Ortho` subdivides into quads → `Weld()` → `Relax()`; for an infinite map, pairing is seeded by a hash of the hex cell, relaxation is deferred and run by a `RelaxModifier` over the whole lazy grid (chunk size 4) so seams smooth; real Townscaper uses some alternative hex generators for variety, a more efficient relaxation, and a rule that favours squarish cells; layers stacked with a prism modifier — [Boris the Brave: Townscaper Grid (Sylves)](https://boristhebrave.com/docs/sylves/1/articles/tutorials/townscaper.html); Red Blob notes quads align because he starts from a triangle grid — [Red Blob mesh quads](https://redblobgames.com/x/1929-mesh-quads)
- Townscaper WFC: adapts Bad North's WFC to real time; each placed block triggers WFC for that cell, decoration is a separate pass, changes ripple through connected structure, WFC "is allowed to fail silently" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Minecraft jigsaw: structures are template pieces connected by jigsaw blocks; "Jigsaw Blocks can specify a Template Pool, which is used to select the next Structure Template"; pools carry weights; generation starts from a start pool with a depth limit; villages still use the "legacy version of the Jigsaw Structure System" not editable via JSON — [Microsoft Creator docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/documents/structures/introductiontojigsawstructures); [Fabric wiki jigsaw tutorial](https://wiki.fabricmc.net/tutorial:1.15:jigsaw)
- Lost Cities (MIT): generates "streets, buildings, parks, highways, bridges, railways, tunnels, and subways"; profile options include `parkChance`, `cityChance` (0.0–1.0), city min/max radius in blocks, `heightSampleSize` (default 1 chunk) for the city heightmap, `grid32` to place spheres on a 32×32-chunk grid instead of 16×16; landscape-correcting/smoothing algorithms fit city chunks to adjacent mountains/ocean — [Lost Cities Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/the-lost-cities); [changelog](https://modrinth.com/mod/the-lost-cities/changelog); [GitHub McJty/LostCities](https://github.com/McJty/LostCities); [Autoloader profile docs](https://www.modpackindex.com/mod/82874/the-lost-cities-autoloader)
- Dwarf Fortress towns: "a large site founded and inhabited by humans"; layout has fortifications and a central keep with the town outside — [DF wiki Town](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Town); worldgen site counts strongly affect history-generation time — [DF2014 Advanced world gen](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Advanced_world_generation)
- Subversion: see Q1 (layered road → classification passes) — [BLDGBLOG](https://bldgblog.com/2009/08/procedural-destruction-and-the-algorithmic-fiction-of-the-city/); project put on hold — [ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/games/subversion-city-generator/images)
- Rust: map listings show monument counts vary per seed/size (e.g. 16–17 on a 4500 map) but no algorithm documentation was found — [just-wiped map listing](https://just-wiped.net/rust-maps/procedural-map-4500-291)

### Inferences
- Townscaper's grid is overkill for a voxel engine (voxels are already a regular grid), but its priority-ordered module selection and "decorate in a second pass" structure are directly reusable for facade/roof detailing.
- Jigsaw pools are the right mental model for town-building on small maps: authored building/street pieces with connector sockets, weights, and a depth limit; it needs no road graph at all for hamlet scale.

### Gaps
- Dwarf Fortress town layout rules (street grid, house placement) are not documented in the sources found (wiki fetch rate-limited).
- Cities: Skylines road tools were not researched (not procedural; out of scope for this pass).
- Rust/Unturned POI placement rules: no developer documentation found.

## Key question 6: Libraries and tools (licences, readability, portability)

### Takeaway
Nothing permissively licensed covers roads+parcels+buildings end to end. Best readable references to port from: ProbableTrain/MapGenerator (TypeScript, LGPL/GPL — read, don't copy), Watabou TownGeneratorOS (Haxe, GPL), Lost Cities (Java, MIT, voxel-native), Chen et al. C++ (no licence), magnificus Procedural-Cities (MIT proof of concept), Sylves (Townscaper grid, C#). CityEngine remains the commercial reference with OBB/skeleton parcels and CGA.

### Cited Findings

## Key question 7: Terrain interaction (uneven terrain, flattening, terracing, bridges, waterfronts, rivers)

### Takeaway
The literature handles terrain in three ways: road rules that follow least-elevation/steepest-ascent (Parish & Müller's San Francisco rule), cost-based path planning with slope/curvature/water costs that decide bridge vs tunnel vs surface (Galin et al. 2010/2012), and interest-map placement of buildings with slope-aware parcels (Galin 2012 villages). Voxel practice (Lost Cities) is per-chunk heightmap sampling plus a smoothing pass to blend city chunks into neighbours.

### Cited Findings
- Parish & Müller San Francisco rule (roads follow least elevation; cross-slope connectors short and steep) and highways flagged to cross water "up to a specified length" to be replaced by bridges; Manhattan example reproduces real bridge positions — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- Galin et al. 2010 roads: path minimising a cost of slope and obstacles (rivers, lakes, forests), then terrain excavated along the path with parameterised road/bridge/tunnel models decided by the same cost — [Galin 2010 roads PDF](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2010-roads.pdf); follow-up builds hierarchical networks between cities with tunnels, bridges, interchanges — [Galin 2011 CGF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Galin11CGF.pdf)
- Galin et al. 2012 villages: buildings seeded by "interest maps" combining sociability, worship, accessibility, slope (bell-shaped preference per building type, construction prohibited outside a slope range), water (close-distance), fortification (open-distance), with per-(village type, building type) weights; roads cost = weighted sum of slope, curvature, water and "crossing an existing building" costs with a re-use weight w_ex < 1 to encourage sharing existing roads; parcels by anisotropic conquest with slope cost; houses built with an "Open Shape Grammar" that adapts floors/footprints to local slope — [Galin 2012 villages PDF](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2012-villages.pdf)
- A* with slope-based cost and a maximum-slope threshold marking nodes acceptable or not (SBGames 2018) — [SBGames 2018 paper](https://www.sbgames.org/sbgames2018/files/papers/ComputacaoFull/188241.pdf)
- BeamNG road editor: conforms terrain to roads; where roads overlap it "terraforms only to the lower road, which produces a natural bridge and embankment"; warns that terraforming many roads at once ignores existing height and can leave floating artefacts — [BeamNG terraforming docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/beamng_tech/roadarchitecteditor/terraforming)
- Lost Cities: `heightSampleSize` heightmap (default per chunk), "landscape correcting algorithm so that city chunks fit much better with adjacent high mountains or very low ocean chunks", smoothing of adjacent city/street chunks, street slopes/connectors and deterministic large bridges — [Lost Cities changelog](https://modrinth.com/mod/the-lost-cities/changelog)
- ProbableTrain: water generated before roads; coast and river become road streamlines; tensors inside water are zero so streets stop at the shore; author suggests keeping tensors near water so minor roads run parallel to it — [MapGenerator docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProbableTrain/MapGenerator/master/docs/algorithmoverview.md)
- Watabou: rivers/coasts came in 0.4.0 and 0.5.0; farms fill outskirts; heightmaps "considered and rejected" — [MFCG 0.4.0](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/4923/040-coastal-cities); [MFCG 0.5.0](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/11802/050-rivers-and-basic-house-shapes); [Some answers](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/1579/some-answers-and-comments)

### Inferences
- For a whole-city generation at world creation, the simplest robust voxel approach is Lost Cities': sample terrain height per lot/chunk cell, assign each block a terrace height (median or min of its cells), flatten lots to it, and let streets between blocks carry the step as slopes/stairs; use the segment-grower's "illegal area" flag to drop bridges where a street crosses a river.
- Galin's cost-weighted roads with w_ex < 1 produce the tree-plus-loops hierarchy naturally and are a good fit for the town-building case on small, hilly maps.

### Gaps
- No source gives numeric slope thresholds for lot terracing in a city context; Galin's per-type slope ranges were not extracted numerically.

## Key question 8: Navigation and semantic data generation should emit (addresses, ownership, doors, road graph)

### Takeaway
Shadows of Doubt is the only shipped example with a documented data hierarchy (City > District > Block > Building > Floor > Address > Room > Tile) where the address is the unit of ownership and the tile grid doubles as the pathfinding and culling structure; academic pipelines emit a street graph (nodes with positions and neighbours), block polygons, and lots with street-access flags, which is the minimum an NPC-routine system needs.

### Cited Findings
- SoD tile grid "supports pathfinding, culling, and generation"; addresses are the ownership unit (apartments, businesses); rooms carry types with door-count and connection rules; entrances are per address and hallways are generated to guarantee reachability — [DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-13-creating-procedural-interiors/)
- SoD citizens have a job, home, favourite places; 4–10 journeys/day planned in a 10–15 s pre-day pass; sightings use line-of-sight between travelling citizens and lit windows — [DevBlog 8](https://colepowered.com/?p=34142)
- Margin City plan carries per-block district, wealth, building and facing; the game generates streets, interiors and residents from that; streets are named entities — [Margin City](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/The_Protectorate/MarginCity/); street gaps typed as main road/back street/alley — [CityPlanner changelog](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/The_Protectorate/CityPlanner/changelog)
- Parish & Müller street graph: crossings create "a new node and two edges in the existing street graph"; lots carry a street-access property used to cull them — [Parish & Müller 2001 PDF](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf)
- ProbableTrain graph: nodes store position and neighbours; blocks found by the right-turn walk — [MapGenerator docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProbableTrain/MapGenerator/master/docs/algorithmoverview.md)
- Watabou exports GeoJSON of the map (buildings, roads), though "ward data is not exported" per a user — [MFCG 0.7.3](https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/112574/073-bug-fixes-geojson-export)
- Galin 2012: accessibility interest and parcel validity both depend on road access; seeds without road access are removed — [Galin 2012 villages PDF](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2012-villages.pdf)

### Inferences
- The generator should persist, independent of voxels: the road graph (nodes, edges with class main/back/alley and width), block polygons with district/wealth, lots with their street-facing edge and door tile, buildings with floors → addresses → rooms (type, door tiles, window tiles), and a per-address owner slot for the population generator. This mirrors SoD's hierarchy and is exactly what its mods manipulate.
- Keeping door positions on the street-facing lot edge (guaranteed by OBB splitting perpendicular to the street) makes the lot→street graph connection trivial for NPC pathing.

### Gaps
- No public documentation of SoD's actual serialised city format (.citb) was found; field-level details are inferred from mod descriptions.
