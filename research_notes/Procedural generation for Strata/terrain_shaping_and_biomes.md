# Terrain shaping and biome architecture for procedural voxel worlds

Scope: how shipped voxel games and well-known write-ups turn raw noise into landforms and biomes. Written for an engine that streams 32-cubed chunks generated by a pure function of (chunk key, seed), and that currently sums low-frequency fBm + hill fBm + mountain-masked ridged noise into a height and fills columns.

Source quality note: the Minecraft numbers below come from the vanilla data files as mirrored in misode/mcmeta (`data` branch, i.e. the *current snapshot* data; field names have been renamed in 26.x — `firstOctave` → `base_octave`, `amplitudes` → `amplitude_modifiers`, `y_clamped_gradient` → `gradient`, `interpolated` → `upscale` — but the values match the 1.18-era design) and from the Minecraft Wiki. Luanti numbers come from `minetest.conf.example` in the engine repo. Where I only have a secondary summary (Kniberg's talk, No Man's Sky, Cube World) I say so.

## Key Question 1: Minecraft's modern (1.18+) pipeline, and the pre-1.18 approach for contrast

### Takeaway
Minecraft 1.18+ samples six low-frequency 2D "climate" noises (temperature, humidity/vegetation, continentalness, erosion, weirdness/ridges, plus a y-derived depth), maps three of them (continents, erosion, folded ridges = "peaks & valleys") through nested cubic splines into three terrain-shaping scalars (offset = surface height, factor = vertical squash, jaggedness = peak noise amplitude), combines those with a y-gradient and a 3D blended noise into a density field (solid where > 0), evaluates it on a 4x8x4 cell grid with trilinear upscaling, then picks biomes independently by nearest-point lookup in the 6D parameter space. Pre-1.18 did the reverse: biomes were decided first (by a layer stack of zoom/island operations), and terrain height was derived from per-biome depth/scale values averaged over a 5x5 kernel.

### Cited Findings

**Climate noises (the "noise router" inputs)**
- The Overworld biome/terrain parameters are temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion, weirdness and depth; all but depth depend only on horizontal position, and depth is roughly 0 at the surface increasing by 1/128 per block downward — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Temperature and humidity affect biome choice only, not terrain shape — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Vanilla noise definitions (base_octave / amplitude_modifiers; frequency of first octave is 2^base_octave in blocks^-1, i.e. -9 ≈ one period per 512 blocks before any xz_scale):
  - continentalness: base_octave -9, amplitudes [1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1] — [mcmeta continentalness.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/continentalness.json)
  - erosion: base_octave -9, amplitudes [1, 1, 0, 1, 1] — [mcmeta erosion.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/erosion.json)
  - ridge (weirdness): base_octave -7, amplitudes [1, 2, 1, 0, 0, 0] — [mcmeta ridge.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/ridge.json)
  - temperature: base_octave -10, amplitudes [1.5, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0] — [mcmeta temperature.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/temperature.json)
  - vegetation (humidity): base_octave -8, amplitudes [1, 1, 0, 0, 0, 0] — [mcmeta vegetation.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/vegetation.json)
  - offset (the domain-warp noise): base_octave -3, amplitudes [1, 1, 1, 0] — [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/offset.json)
- Octave format: `base_octave` is the first octave; `octave_count` 1..32; `amplitude_modifiers` default 1.0 per octave; with `normalize: true`, `base_amplitude` is the expected output amplitude such that 99.7% of samples fall in [-base_amplitude, base_amplitude] — [Minecraft Wiki: Noise](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise)
- A Mojira report notes that when a noise is used for multi-noise biome placement its firstOctave is de facto reduced by 2 (i.e. biome sampling is at quarter resolution) — [MC-239790 via search](https://bugs.mojang.com/browse/MC-239790)
- Domain warp of the climate noises: `overworld/continents` = `cache(noise(minecraft:continentalness, xz_scale 0.25, y_scale 0.0, shift_x = minecraft:shift_x, shift_z = minecraft:shift_z))` — [mcmeta continents.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/continents.json); `shift_x` = `cache(shift_a(noise = minecraft:offset))` — [mcmeta shift_x.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/shift_x.json); `shift`, `shift_a`, `shift_b` sample the noise at quartered coordinates and multiply the result by 4 — [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)
- Peaks-and-valleys is derived from weirdness: PV = 1 − |3·|weirdness| − 2| — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation); the vanilla debug function expresses it as `mul(add(abs(add(abs(ridges), −0.6666667)), −0.33333334), −3.0)` — [mcmeta overworld.json noise settings](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise_settings/overworld.json)

**Splines: offset, factor, jaggedness**
- Three 2D noises (continentalness, erosion, PV) are mapped through splines to a height offset and a vertical stretch factor; higher continentalness raises average height, higher erosion lowers and flattens, higher PV raises terrain, and negative weirdness at high terrain gives taller sharper peaks — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- `spline` evaluates a cubic spline from points with `location`, `value`, `derivative` — [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)
- **Offset** (`overworld/offset`): `cache(lerp(blend_alpha, blend_offset, add(-0.50375, SPLINE_CONT)))`. Outer spline on continents with control points (location → value, derivative 0): −1.1 → 0.044; −1.02 → −0.2222; −0.51 → −0.2222; −0.44 → −0.12; −0.18 → −0.12; then from −0.16, −0.15, −0.1, 0.25, 1.0 the value is itself an erosion spline with locations −0.85, −0.7, −0.4, −0.35, −0.1, 0.2, 0.7 (inland branches add 0.4, 0.45, 0.55, 0.58), whose values are ridges_folded (PV) splines. Representative PV leaves: at far-inland (cont 1.0) low erosion (−0.85): (−1.0 → 0.348, deriv 0), (0.0 → 0.924, deriv 0.576), (1.0 → 1.5, deriv 0.576) — i.e. offset up to 1.5 at peaks; at mid erosion (−0.35) the valley leaf is (−1.0 → −0.2, deriv 0.5), (−0.4 → 0.5), (0 → 0.5), (0.4 → 0.5), (1.0 → 0.6) — a flat plateau with a river cut at PV = −1; at high erosion (0.7) values hover around −0.02…0.1 (flat lowland) — [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)
- Ocean / coast portion of offset: continentalness −1.02…−0.51 → −0.2222 (deep ocean floor), −0.44…−0.18 → −0.12 (shallow ocean), rising through the coast band −0.16…−0.1 — [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)
- **Factor** (`overworld/factor`): `cache(lerp(blend_alpha, 10.0, S_cont))`, all derivatives 0. Continents spline: −0.19 → 3.95; −0.15, −0.1, 0.03, 0.06 → erosion splines. Each erosion spline: at erosion −0.6, −0.35, −0.25, 0.03 → ridges spline {−0.2: 6.3, 0.2: 6.25/5.47/5.08/4.69 (per continent branch)}; at −0.5 → {−0.05: 6.3, 0.05: 2.67}; at −0.1 → {−0.05: 2.67, 0.05: 6.3}; at 0.35 → constant (6.25/5.47/5.08/4.69); at 0.45 and 0.55 → ridges_folded {−0.9: const, −0.69: ridges{0.0: const, 0.1: 0.625}}; at 0.62 → const. The 0.06 branch also has 0.05/0.4 → ridges_folded{0.45: …, 0.7: 1.56} and 0.45/0.55 → {−0.7: …, −0.15: 1.37} — [mcmeta factor.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/factor.json)
- Factor is always positive; it is smaller where windswept savannas or shattered biomes should generate and higher elsewhere, which reduces the influence of base_3d_noise (small factor ⇒ 3D noise dominates ⇒ overhangs/shattered terrain) — [Minecraft Wiki: Tutorial custom world generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation)
- **Jaggedness** (`overworld/jaggedness`): `cache(lerp(blend_alpha, 0.0, S_cont))`; continents −0.11 → 0.0; 0.03 and 0.65 → erosion splines; erosion −1.0, −0.78, −0.5775 → ridges_folded splines {0.2 → 0, 0.45 → 0 (or ridges{−0.01: 0.63, 0.01: 0.3} in the far-inland/low-erosion branch), 1.0 → ridges{−0.01: 0.63, 0.01: 0.3} (0.315/0.15 in the near-inland branch)}; erosion −0.375 → 0.0 — [mcmeta jaggedness.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/jaggedness.json)
- Jaggedness is near 0 almost everywhere, positive only with high continentalness, low erosion and high PV, and larger for negative weirdness; it multiplies `half_negative(noise(minecraft:jagged, xz_scale 1500.0, y_scale 0.0))` — [Minecraft Wiki: Tutorial custom world generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation)

**Density assembly**
- `depth = y_clamped_gradient + offset`, gradient 1.5 at y = −64 to −1.5 at y = 320 (−1/128 per block, 0 at y = 128) — [Minecraft Wiki: Tutorial custom world generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation)
- `sloped_cheese = cache(quarter_negative((depth + cache(jaggedness × half_negative(jagged noise))) × factor) × 4.0 + base_3d_noise)` — [mcmeta sloped_cheese.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/sloped_cheese.json); positive values are multiplied by 4 so deep underground is less affected by base_3d_noise — [Minecraft Wiki: Tutorial](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation)
- `base_3d_noise` = `old_blended_noise(xz_scale 0.25, y_scale 0.125, xz_factor 80.0, y_factor 160.0, smear_scale_multiplier 8.0)` — [mcmeta base_3d_noise.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/base_3d_noise.json)
- `half_negative` halves negative inputs; `quarter_negative` quarters them; `squeeze` clamps to [−1, 1] then applies x/2 − x³/24; `gradient` maps one axis to a range — [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)
- **final_density** (verbatim structure): `add(min(squeeze(upscale(mul(blend_density(lerp(alpha=gradient(y −64→0.0, −40→1.0), first=0.1171875, second=lerp(alpha=gradient(y 240→1.0, 256→0.0), first=−0.078125, second=range_choice(input=sloped_cheese, min −1e6, max_exclusive 1.5625, when_in_range=min(sloped_cheese, caves/entrances × 5.0), when_out_of_range=max(min(min(cave_layer² × 4 + (clamp(cave_cheese + 0.27, −1, 1) + clamp(sloped_cheese × −0.64 + 1.5, 0, 0.5)), caves/entrances), spaghetti_2d + spaghetti_roughness), range_choice(pillars < 0.03 → −1e6 else pillars)))))), 0.64), x cell 4, y cell 8, z cell 4, interpolate_linear)), caves/noodle), beardifier)` — [mcmeta final_density.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/final_density.json)
- Reading of that: the density is forced toward a fixed 0.1171875 (solid) between y −64 and −40 and toward −0.078125 (air) between y 240 and 256; in the "near surface" band (sloped_cheese < 1.5625) only entrance caves cut the terrain; deeper (sloped_cheese ≥ 1.5625) cheese/spaghetti/pillar caves are combined — [Minecraft Wiki: Tutorial](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation)
- The old `interpolated` marker uses cells of `size * 4` configured by cell_size_xz / cell_size_y; its replacement `upscale` samples at reduced resolution along Z, X, then Y with modes from nearest_neighbor to b_spline_cubic and cell size 1–64 — [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)
- Cell size trade-offs stated for `noise`: gravel noise uses xz_scale 2 / y_scale 0; "xz_scale 1 and y_scale 1 produces overhangs, and xz_scale 0.5 smooths the terrain" — [Minecraft Wiki: Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)
- Density above 0 is solid, otherwise air; a height bias squeezes density vertically and a base height shifts the ground up or down; Nether uses two base-height pairs for ceiling+floor; End squeezes terrain into a large island near the bottom — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Overworld preset: sea_level 63, min_y −64, height 384, aquifers enabled; min_y must be in [−2032, 2031] and divisible by 16, height in [0, 4064] divisible by 16, min_y + height ≤ 2032 — [Minecraft Wiki: Noise settings](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_settings)
- Since 26.3 snap7 density functions are evaluated in single precision — [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)
- Terrain "relies on over 50 noise maps" in 1.18+ — [Alan Zucconi: The World Generation of Minecraft](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)

**Multi-noise biome lookup**
- Each biome is defined as intervals in the 6D parameter space; if a location's parameters fall outside every interval the game uses the nearest interval — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Parameter bands — temperature (5): −1.0/−0.45/−0.15/0.2/0.55/1.0; humidity (5): −1.0/−0.35/−0.1/0.1/0.3/1.0; continentalness: −1.2…−1.05 mushroom fields, −1.05…−0.455 deep ocean, −0.455…−0.19 ocean, −0.19…−0.11 coast, −0.11…0.03 near-inland, 0.03…0.3 mid-inland, 0.3…1.0 far-inland; erosion (7): −1.0/−0.78/−0.375/−0.2225/0.05/0.45/0.55/1.0; PV: −1.0…−0.85 valleys, −0.85…−0.2 low, −0.2…0.2 mid, 0.2…0.7 high, 0.7…1.0 peaks — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Non-inland (ocean) biomes depend on continentalness and temperature only; weirdness > 0 yields variants (bamboo jungle, ice spikes) — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Cave biomes use depth: dripstone caves depth 0.2–0.9 & continentalness 0.8–1.0; lush caves depth 0.2–0.9 & humidity 0.7–1.0; deep dark depth 1.1 & erosion −1.0…−0.375 — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Biomes are 3D; each quarter-chunk (4x4x4 blocks) samples the climate parameters and takes the best-matching biome — [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)

**Aquifers**
- Without aquifers all empty space between sea level and y = −54 would flood; below y = −55 is always lava. Each position is Empty, Flooded or "local fluid level". Above the preliminary surface: Flooded. Noise < 0.4 → Empty, > 0.8 → Flooded, in between → local fluid level; near ocean/river the cutoffs shift linearly over the 64 blocks below the preliminary surface (at the surface Empty < −0.8, Flooded > −0.3). Local water-level cells are 16x40x16; water-or-lava cells 64x40x64; above y = −10 always water; a barrier noise separates fluid bodies and a fourth noise sets barrier height so fluids sometimes spill — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Aquifer noises in the router: barrier (xz 1.0, y 0.5), fluid_level_floodedness (xz 1.0, y 0.67), fluid_level_spread (xz 1.0, y 0.714), lava (xz 1.0, y 1.0); exclusion = `min(−0.225 − erosion, max(depth − 0.9, 0))` (i.e. deep-dark region is always dry) — [mcmeta overworld.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise_settings/overworld.json); lava threshold 0.3 — [Minecraft Wiki: Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)
- `chunk_surface_level` (formerly `initial_density_without_jaggedness` → `preliminary_surface_level`) is a 2D density sampled at y = 0 used by aquifers and surface rules and should be lower than the final terrain height — [Minecraft Wiki: Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)

**Surface rules**
- Bedrock: gradient from y −64 to −59 (Java); grass/dirt replace surface blocks, with coarse dirt and podzol gated by noise bands such as surface noise ≥ 7/33 and ≥ 19/165; badlands use terracotta bands by height; snowy/peak biomes choose packed ice, snow, powder snow, calcite, stone by steepness and noise thresholds; deepslate below y 0 with a gradient from y 0 to 8 — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- `surface_rule` was renamed `material_rule` in 26.3 snap 1; `default_block` removed in 26.4 snap 1 so all blocks now come from material rules — [Minecraft Wiki: Noise settings](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_settings)

**Ore veins**
- Three noises toggle/ridge/gap; toggle is 0 outside y −60…51; negative toggle → iron, positive → copper; gap sets ore:filler ratio 10–30%; 98% normal ore, 2% raw ore block — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)

**Pre-1.18 (Beta 1.8 – 1.17) for contrast**
- Biomes came first via "layer stacks": an Island layer where ~1 in 10 pixels is land with each pixel covering 4,096 blocks; Zoom layers double resolution and add random edge errors; AddIsland/Zoom alternate; "Remove Too Much Ocean" gives each deep-ocean cell a 50% chance of land (land ~27% → >50%); climate assigned Warm/Cold/Freezing 4:1:1 with incompatible neighbours blended; rivers from a separate stack by edge-detecting a scaled noise; final map upscaled twice with a Voronoi-style technique to 1 pixel per block — [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)
- Terrain: fBm density sampled from y 255 downward, first y with noise ≥ 0 is the surface; sampled in 4x8x4 cells for speed; three fractal noises, two blended by a third, plus separate Depth and Surface noises; each biome sets a depth (average height) and scale (variation), linking biome to terrain from Beta 1.8 onward — [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)
- Legacy Console Edition docs (same lineage): density sampled every 4 blocks horizontally and 8 vertically on a 5x17x5 grid; a 5x5 neighbourhood of biomes around each column, with distance weighting applied to each biome's depth and scale values via a precomputed kernel — [coah80 LCE docs via search](https://coah80.github.io/lce-docs/world/worldgen) (page itself returned 404 on fetch; claim is from the search snippet)
- Customized-world era code: `maxHeight = biomeScaleOffset + biome.maxHeight * biomeScaleWeight`; Biome Depth Weight range 1.0–20.0 default 1.0 — [MC-54738 via search](https://mojira.dev/MC-54738)
- Legacy misode gist (20w-era) shows the pre-density-function noise block: `top_slide {target −3000, size 64, offset −46}`, `bottom_slide {target −30, size 7, offset 1}`, `sampling {xz_scale 2.0, y_scale 1.0, xz_factor 80.0, y_factor 160.0}`, `size_horizontal 2, size_vertical 1` — [misode gist](https://gist.github.com/misode/44d11ce8d0fb89648366df48de5e78fb)
- 1.18 introduced biome blending so there are no harsh borders between new and old terrain; surface height and biomes blend while caves may vary — [minecraft.net 1.18 pre-release 1 via search](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-1-18-pre-release-1); old chunks surrounded by ≥12 generated chunks still get biome borders slightly changed (Works As Intended) — [MC-244340](https://bugs.mojang.com/browse/MC-244340)
- Kniberg's talk is the canonical narration of continentalness/erosion/PV + splines; secondary summaries say: "Continentalness could be thought of as how inland a part of the world is, whilst Erosion describes how flat an area will be"; high erosion → plains, low erosion → cliffs/mountains; PV creates rivers between mountains/plateaus — [flauschbert devlog](https://flauschbert.itch.io/minecraftian-voxel-engine-demo/devlog/549859/minecraftian-voxel-engine-1-terrain-height-generation), [Dawnosaur](https://dawnosaur.substack.com/p/how-minecraft-generates-worlds-you); video links: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CSa5O6knuwI and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TycBrFKEteU — [Enhanced-Procedural-Terrain-Generation README](https://github.com/AndrzejKebab/Enhanced-Procedural-Terrain-Generation)

### Inferences
- The architecture is "terrain parameters first, biomes second, both from the same noises" — biome and landform agree because they read the same continentalness/erosion/PV values, without the biome ever dictating height. This is what lets a mountain biome always sit on mountainous terrain without cross-chunk biome lookups.
- The spline nesting order (continents → erosion → PV) is itself a gating hierarchy: ocean floor height is fixed regardless of erosion/PV; only the inland branches expose the full erosion×PV detail. Your "mountain mask" is the degenerate one-level version of this.
- `factor` is Minecraft's answer to "how 3D is the terrain here": it scales the signed height-difference term before adding 3D noise, so a factor of ~6 makes the surface nearly a heightmap, while ~0.6–1.5 (shattered/windswept) lets 3D noise punch through.
- The 4x8x4 cell evaluation plus trilinear upscaling is what makes a 3D density affordable; the price is that features smaller than a cell are smeared. Your 32³ chunk divides evenly into 8x4x8 cells of 4x8x4.
- Everything in the router is a pure function of (x, y, z, seed): the only chunk-level coupling is blending with legacy chunks (`blend_alpha/blend_offset/blend_density`) and the `beardifier` (structure terrain adaptation).

## Key Question 2: Other shipped or open examples (Luanti/Minetest, Terasology, Veloren, Hytale, Cube World, Vintage Story, Dwarf Fortress, No Man's Sky, Astroneer)

### Takeaway
The field splits into (a) pure-noise, chunk-local generators that stack 2D heights with 3D "mountain" or "cave" noises (Luanti v5/v6/v7/carpathian/valleys, Hytale, Astroneer), (b) landform/biome-catalogue generators where a low-frequency selector noise picks an authored terrain recipe per region and blends recipes at borders (Vintage Story, Hytale biomes, Terasology's facet graph), and (c) offline world-simulation generators that run erosion/climate on a coarse grid once, then expand to voxels per chunk (Veloren, Dwarf Fortress). Only (c) can produce consistent river networks; (a) and (b) stay strictly chunk-local.

### Cited Findings

**Luanti (Minetest) mapgens** — all values are `{offset, scale, (spread xyz), seed, octaves, persistence, lacunarity, flags}`, from [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example)

**Terasology**
- Facet-based world generation: "Build up information about the landscape and its features from multiple facets" ranging "from dense height maps to sparse object configurations"; facet providers form a graph allowing reuse/composition; each rasterizer "interprets facet data to decide on the type and shape of a block to place"; modules can add lakes, caves, volcanoes by adding providers and rasterizers — [TutorialWorldGeneration wiki](https://github.com/Terasology/TutorialWorldGeneration/wiki)

**Veloren (Rust)**
- Three stages: Geological (noise generates altitude, rock strength, humidity, temperature; an erosion algorithm runs on them; output altitude map saved as a `.bin` asset), Filling (at server start: lakes/rivers, whether depressions fill with liquid, tree density, dunes), Reshaping (local features: cliffs, caves; caves and paths use a shared "way" system that connects long elements across chunks) — [Veloren book: Worldgen](https://book.veloren.net/internals/worldgen/worldgen.html)
- Erosion postmortem: fluvial erosion computes the drainage network explicitly then solves shear stress implicitly (large time steps), erosion strength ∝ water volume (approximated by upstream area); tectonic uplift so mountains form from flat ground; thermal erosion breaks rock steeper than its talus angle (rock-type dependent) which limits altitude under uplift; fully implicit hillslope diffusion ("converges for every time scale") makes hills convex, gentles beaches, fills depressions, creates oxbow lakes; noise still sets uplift rates, initial continent shape, rock strength and diffusion rate, and drives humidity/temperature maps weighted by altitude and flux — [Veloren devblog 43](https://veloren.net/devblog-43)
- "Nobody has ever been able to figure out a way to make river networks that fit the terrain with pure noise" — [Veloren devblog 43](https://veloren.net/devblog-43); uplift and erosion reach a steady state so erosion can run indefinitely without flattening — [Veloren devblog 36 via search](https://veloren.net/devblog-30)
- Mountain/river generation draws on Cordonnier et al., "Large Scale Terrain Generation from Tectonic Uplift and Fluvial Erosion" — [makeitbigingames community page](https://makeitbigingames.com/open-source/veloren/)
- Pipeline resolution: world generation, town generation and world simulation at low resolution, then chunk generation at block resolution; terrain chunks are 32x32x16 voxels subdivided into 4x4x4 groups; `WorldSim` holds a vector of `SimChunk` with `map_size_lg` (dimensions as base-2 log in chunks, must be powers of 2) — [docs.veloren.net WorldSim](https://docs.veloren.net/veloren_world/sim/struct.WorldSim.html), [veloren_common chunk docs](https://docs.veloren.net/veloren_common/volumes/chunk/index.html)

**Hytale**
- Terrain is "founded on this concept of Density fields": 3D decimal fields, positive = solid, negative = air, "typically between 1 and −1", computed per coordinate as a sum (`f(x) = z + y`); basic terrain = "combining a set of heights with a noise field" via Terrain / Simplex 2D / Y-Curve nodes; function nodes like Absolute (ridged) and Normalization (remap range, e.g. move 0 to −1 so half the field is solid) — [Hytale modding: density concepts](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/worldgen-tutorial/density-generation-concepts)
- Node catalogue: SimplexNoise2D (cached per column) / 3D with Lacunarity 2.0, Persistence 0.5, Scale 50, Octaves 4 (">10 uncommon"); PositionsCellNoise (Voronoi) with CellValue/Density/Curve/Distance returns and MaxDistance "a bit over half the spacing"; shapes (Ellipsoid, Cylinder, Plane, Shell…); Mix/MultiMix/SmoothMin/SmoothMax (Range 0.2)/Clamp/CurveMapper/Normalizer; GradientWarp (expensive, SampleRange 1, WarpFactor), FastGradientWarp (internal simplex: WarpScale/Lacunarity/Persistence/Octaves/WarpFactor = max displacement), VectorWarp, PositionsPinch, PositionsTwist; YSampled (sample every N blocks along Y and interpolate; SampleDistance 4 is a "safe start"; TerrainStage timings 14 ms no sampling, 8 ms at 4, 6 ms at 8, 5 ms at 16 — gains large for 3D noise, small for 2D); Cache (Capacity 3); Multiplier short-circuits on 0 so cheap masks go first (~40% speedup reported); DistanceToBiomeEdge node; BaseHeight node referencing a WorldStructure constant — [Hytale modding: density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density)
- World structure: a `Density` (default constant 1) drives biome distribution and a list of `Biomes` delimiters assigns a biome where the density falls within bounds; `DefaultBiome` elsewhere; `DefaultTransitionDistance` 32 blocks ("terrain blends between neighboring biomes across this distance", larger hurts performance); `MaxBiomeEdgeDistance` (default 0; beyond it DistanceFromBiomeEdge reads −1); a Framework of named Positions and DecimalConstants (e.g. `Base` = 100) shared so biomes stay consistent — [Hytale modding: world structure](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/world-structure)
- 2020 blog (marked outdated): noisemaps + thresholds shape zones ("Adjusting thresholds can turn a winding waterway into a river canyon"); Layers set block depth/surface type; Covers are grass/foliage per layer; Voronoi cells decide where large prefabs may appear (~20% of space in the example); the node editor "does not currently support noise visualization" — [Hytale: landscape generation](https://www.hytale.com/news/2020/6/a-closer-look-at-landscape-generation-in-hytale), [Hytale modding: density concepts](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/worldgen-tutorial/density-generation-concepts)

## Key Question 3: Biome determination techniques and blending

### Takeaway
Three determination families are in use: lookup tables over a few climate axes (Whittaker-style temperature × moisture; Luanti's Voronoi in heat/humidity space; Minecraft's nearest-interval in 6D), region partition (Voronoi cells with jittered seeds — Hytale prefabs, Vintage Story mods, Red Blob's polygon maps), and climate simulation (latitude/altitude/rainfall — Vintage Story, Dwarf Fortress, Red Blob mapgen4). Seams are avoided either by making terrain *not* depend on the biome (Minecraft 1.18) or by blending the biome's terrain parameters over a kernel/edge distance (Hytale 32-block transition, pre-1.18 Minecraft 5x5 kernel, community distance-weighted averages).

### Cited Findings
- Whittaker-style table: use a second noise map with a different seed for moisture and a lookup table keyed on elevation and moisture, "loosely based on Whittaker's system"; elevation alone only produces bands; use separate seeds or the maps will be identical — [Red Blob: Making maps with noise](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)
- Luanti: biome = Voronoi cell containing (heat, humidity) with `weight` scaling cell size; plus `y_min/y_max` and `vertical_blend`; heat/humidity are 1000-spread, 3-octave noises (offset 50, scale 50) with an 8-spread "blend" noise (scale 1.5, 2 octaves) added — [Luanti API](https://api.luanti.org/definition-tables/), [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example)
- Minecraft: nearest interval in 6D; biome sampled per 4x4x4 cell; ocean biomes ignore humidity/erosion/weirdness; weirdness sign selects variants — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation), [Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)
- Voronoi regions: Hytale uses Voronoi cells to decide where large prefabs can occur — [Hytale blog](https://www.hytale.com/news/2020/6/a-closer-look-at-landscape-generation-in-hytale); Hytale's PositionsCellNoise returns CellValue (constant per cell sampled at the cell core) or a per-cell Density chosen by a ChoiceDensity — [Hytale density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density); a thesis on procedural islands uses Voronoi noise to set biome position and size with smooth transitions related to nearby biomes — [diposit.ub.edu via search](https://diposit.ub.edu/bitstreams/1900f2af-252d-4954-9ad7-903c582be3eb/download)
- Red Blob mapgen2 used Delaunay triangulation (mapbox/delaunator) with biomes "based on distance to coastline and distance to water" — [Red Blob mapgen2](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/mapgen2/); mapgen4 simulates "evaporation, wind, and rainfall to determine biomes and rivers" on a Delaunay/Voronoi mesh with poisson-disk sampling, generating user-painted elevation first and biomes/rivers after — [Red Blob mapgen4](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/mapgen4/)
- Climate simulation: Vintage Story climate from latitude, altitude, humidity (hexagon-pyramid diagram) — [VS wiki](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Terrain_Generation); Luanti valleys `altitude_chill 90` makes cold biomes more common up high — [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example); DF temperature from latitude + elevation — [DF wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/40d:World)
- Pre-1.18 Minecraft climate layers: Warm/Cold/Freezing assigned 4:1:1, incompatible adjacent climates blended to Temperate/Cold; rare biome rolls (1 in 13 climate regions special, 1 in 100 surrounded ocean → mushroom island) — [Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)
- Blending by kernel (pre-1.18): 5x5 neighbourhood of biomes around each column, each biome's depth/scale distance-weighted by a precomputed kernel — [LCE docs via search](https://coah80.github.io/lce-docs/world/worldgen)
- Blending by edge distance (Hytale): `DefaultTransitionDistance` 32 blocks; `DistanceToBiomeEdge` node outputs distance to nearest biome edge in blocks — [Hytale world structure](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/world-structure)
- Community reports: a linear blend still left cliff-like borders; what worked was weighting each neighbouring biome's height by distance and dividing by total weight, though cliffs return when biome selection depends on temperature rather than height — [gamedev.stackexchange via search](https://backiee.wasmer.app/https_gamedev_stackexchange_com/a/194432); hard borders came from different per-biome terrain scale parameters, fixed with ease functions — [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/need-assistance-with-blending-biomes/117823); Planetation stores edge/neighbour data and blends along shared edges — [80.lv Planetation](https://80.lv/articles/learn-how-space-survival-planetation-redefined-voxel-biomes/); one voxel dev places features over a temp/moisture range "that encompasses the biome, but extends a little past" so foliage overlaps neighbours — [r/VoxelGameDev](https://nyc1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/VoxelGameDev/comments/1gq0nnw/biome_and_terrain_generation_whats_your_approach)
- Luanti v6 has an explicit "biome blending" flag that smooths transitions — [Luanti docs: features](https://docs.luanti.org/for-creators/mapgen/features/)
- Minecraft 1.18 blends surface height and biomes between new and old chunks (`blend_alpha`, `blend_offset`, `blend_density` nodes) — [minecraft.net](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-1-18-pre-release-1), [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)

### Inferences
- Seams come from a *discontinuous* map from position to terrain parameters. Minecraft 1.18 removes the discontinuity at the source: terrain parameters are continuous splines of continuous noise, and the discrete biome ID only drives surface material and features, which can tolerate hard edges. This is the lowest-cost way to be seam-free in a chunk-local generator — no neighbour sampling needed.
- If terrain style must be per-biome (Vintage Story / Luanti v6 / your current design), the parameters that feed height (amplitudes, base height, roughness) must be blended, not the heights themselves; kernel radius must exceed the largest height difference's "cliff" width, and the kernel must sample the biome function at absolute world coordinates so adjacent chunks agree.
- Consistency of a biome's style: in the spline approach it comes for free because the same (cont, ero, PV) region always maps to the same offset/factor/jaggedness; in the catalogue approach it comes from the landform definition itself. The danger case is a biome selected by an axis the terrain ignores (temperature), which is exactly the Stack Exchange "cliffs come back" failure.
- Hybrid used by several engines: selector noise → discrete recipe for *style* (e.g. 10-octave amplitude vector), continuous blend of recipe *parameters* across a distance; this keeps authored variety while avoiding seams.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gives the pre-1.18 kernel weights numerically.
- Hytale's blending math (how the 32-block transition weights neighbouring biome densities) is not documented on the pages I reached.

## Key Question 4: Splines and masks — mapping noise to height/amplitude, gating layers, coarse-to-fine hierarchy

### Takeaway
Shipped generators almost never use raw fBm as height. They remap it (Red Blob's exponent, Minecraft's cubic splines with explicit derivatives, Vintage Story's Y-key density profile) and gate higher-frequency layers by lower-frequency selectors (Minecraft's nested continents→erosion→PV splines; Luanti's `height_select`, `mount_height`, `terrain_persist`; carpathian's three selector noises). Scale hierarchy in practice: continent-scale 500–2000 blocks, hill/ridge-scale 100–600, detail 10–60.

### Cited Findings
- Redistribution: `pow(e, exponent)`, higher exponents push mid-level terrain down into flat valleys; a fudge factor ~1.2 applied first — [Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)
- Minecraft splines: cubic with `location`, `value`, `derivative` per point; nested so a spline's value may be another spline on a different coordinate — [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function), [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)
- Gating example from offset: ocean branch (cont ≤ −0.18) returns constants regardless of erosion/PV; jaggedness returns 0 for cont ≤ −0.11 or erosion ≥ −0.375, and the full 0.63 peak amplitude only for cont ≥ 0.65 & erosion ≤ −0.5775 & PV ≥ 0.45 & weirdness < 0 — [mcmeta jaggedness.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/jaggedness.json)
- Minecraft scale hierarchy: continentalness/erosion base octave −9 (~512-block first octave, with shift sampling at ×0.25 = effectively ~2048), ridge −7, vegetation −8, temperature −10, jagged noise xz_scale 1500, offset (warp) noise −3 — [mcmeta noise files](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/continentalness.json)
- Luanti v7 hierarchy: terrain_persist spread 2000 → terrain_base/alt spread 600 (5 octaves, lacunarity 2 ⇒ finest ~37) → height_select 500 → mount_height 1000 selecting 3D mountain noise spread (250, 350, 250) → ridge 100; carpathian: selectors 1301/1889, heights 251–631, shapes 257/509/743 — [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example)
- Luanti v6: `height_select {0.5, 1, 250…}` blends terrain_base (−4 ± 20, spread 250) and terrain_higher (20 ± 16, spread 500), `steepness {0.85 ± 0.5, spread 125}` — [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example)
- Vintage Story: per-landform 10-entry octave amplitude vector + octave thresholds (gate an octave so it is active only part of the time) + Y-key density profile — [VS WorldGen Configuration](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Configuration)
- Hytale: CurveMapper/Curves remap any density; Y-Curve node "a 2D curve drawn between a height differential"; Mix/MultiMix with Gauge; Multiplier used as mask with short-circuit on 0 — [Hytale density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density)
- Octave weights: Red Blob uses amplitudes 1, 1/2, 1/4 or 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, normalised by the amplitude sum; Minecraft's amplitude arrays are arbitrary per octave (e.g. continentalness [1,1,2,2,2,1,1,1,1] weights the mid octaves; erosion [1,1,0,1,1] skips one) — [Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/), [mcmeta](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/erosion.json)
- Minecraft's climate noises are listed with "firstOctave de facto reduced by 2" when sampled for biomes — [MC-239790](https://bugs.mojang.com/browse/MC-239790)

### Inferences
- A spline with derivatives is a strictly more expressive "mask": your `mountain_mask * ridged` is `offset = spline_cont(...)` with a two-point spline. Moving to splines lets you express plateaus (flat segment), cliffs (steep segment), river cuts (a notch at PV = −1) and coasts (a step at cont ≈ −0.19…−0.11) in the same table.
- Gating by nesting (outer coordinate chooses which inner spline) is cheaper and more controllable than multiplying masks, because each branch is an authored curve rather than a product of two unrelated noises.
- The 3-to-4-level scale ladder is consistent across engines: ~1–2 km selector, ~500 m base height, ~100–300 m hills/ridges, ~10–60 m detail. Octave *skipping* (Minecraft erosion [1,1,0,1,1], VS thresholds) is used to decorrelate layers and leave "room" for a gating layer at the skipped scale.

### Gaps
- None of the sources gives a published rationale for the specific Minecraft control-point values; they are tuned by hand.

## Key Question 5: Domain warping, ridged noise, plateaus/terraces, coastlines/sea level/beaches/cliffs

### Takeaway
Domain warping (f(p + k·fbm(p + …))) is the standard way to break the "noise look" and is used by Minecraft (climate shift noises), Hytale (GradientWarp/FastGradientWarp/VectorWarp), No Man's Sky and most heightmap tools; ridged noise is `2·(0.5 − |0.5 − n|)` (Red Blob) or `abs` (Hytale) with higher octaves scaled by the previous octave; terraces are quantisation `round(e·n)/n` optionally smoothed; coasts fall out of a continent noise band around sea level, with beaches/cliffs driven by a density-profile knee near sea level (Vintage Story) or surface rules (Minecraft).

### Cited Findings
- Domain warp: replace f(p) with f(g(p)); keep the distortion mild; two-level form `q = (fbm(p + (0,0)), fbm(p + (5.2,1.3)))`, `r = (fbm(p + 4·q + (1.7,9.2)), fbm(p + 4·q + (8.3,2.8)))`, `return fbm(p + 4·r)`; the offset constants are arbitrary decorrelators; compact form `fbm(p + fbm(p + fbm(p)))` — [Inigo Quilez: warp](https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp) (formula confirmed via [Book of Shaders ch.13](https://thebookofshaders.com/13/)); warp strength is measured in noise-lattice units — [GameDev.net shaderlab](https://gamedev.net/shaderlab/23-domain-warped-noise/)
- Minecraft applies warp to the climate noises via `shift_x/shift_z` (shift_a/shift_b of the `offset` noise at quartered coordinates ×4); the 26.4 snapshot adds a dedicated `domain_warp` density function — [mcmeta continents.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/continents.json), [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)
- Hytale: GradientWarp (gradient of a second density, SampleRange 1, expensive), FastGradientWarp (WarpFactor = max displacement in blocks), VectorWarp (displacement = WarpFactor × field, e.g. 15 × 0.5 = 7.5 blocks); upstream warping reduces YSampled interpolation gains — [Hytale density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density)
- No Man's Sky (secondary): domain warping for overhangs/twisted terrain, "filtering" to emphasise ridges/valleys and suppress bland regions — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/dubeykartikay/how-no-mans-sky-creates-18-quintillion-planets-with-just-math-3fgf)
- Ridged: `2·(0.5 − |0.5 − noise|)`; scale higher octaves by the previous octave's result so detail appears mainly on mountains — [Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/); Hytale Absolute node "producing a ridged shape on a simplex field" — [Hytale density concepts](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/worldgen-tutorial/density-generation-concepts); Minecraft's PV = 1 − |3|w| − 2| is a folded (ridged) transform of the weirdness noise giving two ridges per period, and `half_negative(jagged)` keeps only sharp positive peaks — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation), [Minecraft Wiki: Tutorial](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation)
- Terraces: `round(e·n)/n`; non-step functions can make rounder or selective terraces — [Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/); Luanti carpathian has "terraced mountains" via `np_step_mnt` and `np_step_terrain` — [Luanti docs](https://docs.luanti.org/for-creators/mapgen/), [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example); Minecraft's offset spline has flat PV plateaus (e.g. values 0.35 or 0.5 across PV −0.4…0.4 at mid-erosion) which read as mesas — [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)
- Coastlines: Minecraft continentalness bands coast −0.19…−0.11, ocean −0.455…−0.19, deep ocean −1.05…−0.455 and offset −0.2222 (deep) / −0.12 (shallow) rising through the coast band; sea_level 63 — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation), [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)
- Island shaping: `d` = distance from centre, `e = lerp(e, 1 − d, mix)` — [Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)
- Beaches/cliffs via vertical profile: "Sudden changes in position at similar density create cliffs. Sharp density changes near sea level create flat beaches"; default sea level at Y-position 0.43 — [VS WorldGen Configuration](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Configuration); Luanti v6 `np_beach {0, 1, 250…}` selects sand beaches; pre-1.18 Minecraft "everything below y = 62 that is empty becomes water" — [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example), [Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)
- Luanti valleys rivers: `np_rivers` spread 256 thresholded with `river_size 5`, `river_depth 4`, valley profile/depth noises; v7 "ridges" rivers from `np_ridge_uwater` (spread 1000) and `np_ridge` (100) — [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example)
- Minecraft rivers are the PV = −1 valley band (−1.0…−0.85) with the offset spline notching to −0.2…−0.3 there — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation), [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)

### Inferences
- Rivers in chunk-local generators are always "ridge-of-a-noise" constructs (Minecraft PV valleys, Luanti ridge/rivers noises): they are level with the sea (Minecraft/Luanti v7) or need a special non-flowing liquid (Luanti valleys) because a noise ridge has no notion of downhill. Real drainage needs Veloren/DF-style global simulation.
- Warping the *selector* noises (as Minecraft does) is cheaper than warping the detail noises and yields organic coastlines and biome borders at low frequency; warping detail noise is what gives overhangs (Hytale/NMS).

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the Quilez page directly (redirect loop); formula is as reported by secondary sources and matches the well-known article.

## Key Question 6: Chunk-local purity — keeping generation a pure function of chunk coordinates, and where engines cheat

### Takeaway
All the noise-driven engines evaluate terrain as a pure function of world (x, y, z, seed), so any chunk can be generated alone; the cheats are (1) features/structures that overhang chunk borders, handled by Minecraft with a staged pipeline where features may write into a 3x3 chunk area and structures into up to 17x17, (2) blending against pre-existing chunks, and (3) precomputed global data (Veloren's baked erosion map, DF's world map) that is read-only at chunk time.

### Cited Findings
- Minecraft stages: noise (base terrain + fluids), surface, carvers, features, initialize_light; carvers "only have their own chunk to work on and cannot look into or modify surrounding chunks"; features "can at most write to a 3x3 chunk area centered on the placement chunk", structures "a much bigger area" with a hard limit of 17x17 chunks; features/structures begin only once all chunks in the area are carved — [Minecraft Wiki World generation via search](https://wiki.ronlab.site/content/minecraftwiki_en_all_maxi_2025-11/World_Generation), [CorgiTaco gist](https://gist.github.com/CorgiTaco/96f968881636ceaa498101478a50fe4d)
- Chunks may reach the feature stage with different sets of features applied, so crossing chunk boundaries can produce odd results (community observation) — [XI64 gist](https://gist.github.com/XI64/0480256ed2836e4d301210899551c659)
- Density functions: `cache` computes once per block position; `interpolated`/`upscale` cell sampling is per-chunk aligned (cells 4/8/4); `beardifier` is "added by the game and shouldn't be referenced in data packs" (structure terrain adaptation) — [Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)
- Blending with legacy chunks is explicit in the function graph: `lerp(alpha = blend_alpha, first = blend_offset/10.0/0.0, second = spline)` in offset/factor/jaggedness and `blend_density` in final_density — [mcmeta offset/factor/jaggedness/final_density](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/final_density.json)
- Aquifers use local cells (16x40x16 water level, 64x40x64 water-vs-lava) so fluid levels are consistent across chunk borders without communication — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Astroneer: "layered noise functions to create terrain and place objects as needed, without knowing about neighboring sections" — [Unreal spotlight](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/spotlights/how-system-era-softworks-leveraged-ue4-to-create-astroneer-s-wonderful-universe)
- Hytale's SimplexNoise2D is "cached per column"; Cache node capacity 3; biome edge distance tracked only up to `MaxBiomeEdgeDistance` — [Hytale density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density), [world structure](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/world-structure)
- Veloren: geological stage output baked to a `.bin` asset; "way" system connects caves/paths across chunks — [Veloren book](https://book.veloren.net/internals/worldgen/worldgen.html)
- Terasology: rasterizers must stay within the bounds of the data available to them (facet borders) — [Terasology forum](https://forum.terasology.org/threads/shattered-planes.1492/post-13225)
- Seam example: generating each chunk's heightmap separately produced 1 m seams until "the issue causing the same points in the heightmap not being consistent" was fixed — [varkxel Utopia blog](https://varkxel.github.io/utopia/blog/entry8.html)
- Luanti mapgens generate in 80-node mapchunks (5x5x5 mapblocks of 16) and expose `heightmap`, `biomemap`, `heatmap` objects to Lua mapgens (mentioned in API navigation; details not fetched) — [Luanti API](https://api.luanti.org/definition-tables/)

### Inferences
- Pure-function purity is easy for density/height: just never read chunk memory, only world coordinates. The three things that break it are (a) kernel blending (reads biome at x±r — still pure if biome() is a pure function of world coords, just more expensive), (b) features larger than a chunk (solve by deterministic per-region seeding: hash(region key, seed) decides the feature; every chunk that overlaps re-derives the same decision and writes only its own part), (c) anything that depends on "what was generated before" (legacy blending) — avoid entirely in a fresh engine.
- Minecraft's 3x3 feature stage is a *scheduling* cheat, not a determinism cheat: feature placement is still seeded per chunk; the pipeline merely delays the stage until neighbours exist so it can write into them. An engine that wants strict purity can instead let each chunk query "which features from the 3x3 neighbourhood intersect me" and rasterise only the intersecting part.

### Gaps
- No fetched source documents Minecraft's exact per-chunk/per-feature RNG seeding (population seed, feature index salting); the "deterministic per-region seeding" pattern is inferred.

## Key Question 7: Vertical world extent and multiple vertical chunks

### Takeaway
Engines with a true 3D density (Minecraft, Hytale, Luanti v5/v7 mountains) evaluate it per (x, y, z) and so can generate any y-slab independently, with caches keyed per column for the 2D inputs; heightmap engines (Luanti v6, pre-1.18) are per-column by construction. The practical pattern is: compute 2D terms once per column (cheap, cacheable), then evaluate the y-dependent density only within the y-range of the chunk being generated, with a y-gradient term that makes density trivially solid far below and air far above.

### Cited Findings
- Minecraft Overworld is y −64…320 (height 384); depth gradient 1.5 → −1.5 across that range (0 at y 128); `gradient` clamps outside its range; final density pins to constants below y −40 and above y 240 — [Minecraft Wiki: Noise settings](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_settings), [Minecraft Wiki: Tutorial](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation), [mcmeta final_density.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/final_density.json)
- Biomes are 3D per 4x4x4 cell with depth as the sixth axis (1/128 per block) enabling cave biomes at depth 0.2–1.1 — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Aquifer local-level cells are 40 blocks tall, independent of chunk sections — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Luanti floatlands y 1024–4096 in v7 (separate noise `np_floatland` spread (384, 96, 384)), lava below y −273 (v5), caverns 3D noise spread (384, 128, 384) — [minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example), [Luanti docs](https://docs.luanti.org/for-creators/mapgen/)
- Vintage Story: Y-key positions are relative (0 bottom, 1 top), with "more than 256 thresholds pointless for a 256-block world height" — [VS WorldGen Configuration](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Configuration)
- Hytale: SimplexNoise2D cached per column; YSampled interpolates along Y at SampleDistance 4–16; the Nether-style "ceiling and floor" uses two base-height pairs — [Hytale density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density), [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)
- Veloren chunks are 32x32x16 columns stacked on a 2D sim grid — [veloren_common chunk docs](https://docs.veloren.net/veloren_common/volumes/chunk/index.html)
- Astroneer's "cave layers" are separated from surface biomes as additional layers; some caves run from surface to outer core — [Unreal spotlight](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/spotlights/how-system-era-softworks-leveraged-ue4-to-create-astroneer-s-wonderful-universe)

### Inferences
- With 32³ chunks, generating a y-chunk independently costs re-evaluating the 2D column terms (offset/factor/jaggedness/biome) once per y-chunk; this is wasted work for tall columns, so engines cache per-column 2D results (Hytale "cached per column", Minecraft `cache_2d`/`flat_cache` historically) keyed by (chunk x, z).
- A y-gradient term (density += k·(y_ref − y)) is what lets a y-chunk far above or below the surface early-out to all-air/all-solid without touching noise: evaluate the 2D surface estimate first, compare with the chunk's y range ± the 3D noise amplitude / factor, and skip.
- Cell-based upscaling must be aligned to world y multiples of the cell height (8) so that stacked y-chunks share cell corners; a 32-tall chunk is 4 cells of 8.

### Gaps
- No source describes an engine that generates cubic chunks with a 3D density and explicitly discusses per-y-chunk cost; Minecraft generates whole columns (all 24 sections) at once.

## Key Question 8: Tuning workflows — visualising noise layers, parameter tweaking, world preview tools

### Takeaway
Every mature generator ships or relies on a 2D visualiser for its noise layers and a fast "regenerate the area" loop; Minecraft's ecosystem uses Misode's density-function/noise generators and the wiki's documented parameter bands, Vintage Story has an in-game regen command and a web tool for Y-profiles, Luanti has a Perlin Explorer mod, Red Blob provides interactive sliders, and Hytale's node editor notably lacks noise visualisation (called out as a limitation).

### Cited Findings
- Red Blob: tune biome thresholds per project; normalise output to a known range; use separate seeds per map; "the value distribution often matters more than the nominal range"; visualise as you go with the page's sliders, 2D/3D views, and the Noise Studio — [Red Blob](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)
- Minecraft: Misode's generators recommended for producing worldgen JSON; the wiki's Noise page points to the Noise Generator on misode.github.io; multinoise example on misode's deepslate — [PlanetMinecraft docs via search](https://www.planetminecraft.com/blog/custom-world-generation-documentation/), [Minecraft Wiki: Noise](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise), [misode deepslate multinoise](https://misode.github.io/deepslate/examples/multinoise/)
- Minecraft debug density function for PV exists in the noise settings (`mul(add(abs(add(abs(ridges), −0.667)), −0.333), −3)`) — [mcmeta overworld.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise_settings/overworld.json)
- Vintage Story: `/wgen autogen 0`, `/wgen del 10`, `/wgen regen 5` reload landforms.json and regenerate without restart; a world-gen tool at tyron.at builds Y-key graphs — [VS WorldGen Configuration](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Configuration)
- Luanti: the Perlin Explorer mod is "useful to discover useful noise parameters for the various mapgen settings"; all mapgen params live in advanced settings / minetest.conf.example — [Perlin Explorer README via search](https://git.minetest.io/Wuzzy/minetest_perlin_explorer_2025/blame/branch/master/README.md), [Luanti docs](https://docs.luanti.org/for-creators/mapgen/)
- Hytale: "the Hytale Node Editor does not currently support noise visualization" — [Hytale density concepts](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/worldgen-tutorial/density-generation-concepts); the Multiplier/mask-ordering optimisation and YSampled timings were reported as measured TerrainStage times — [Hytale density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density)
- Red Blob mapgen4: real-time regeneration is required because painting needs it; multithreading keeps it interactive; "shape the elevation to match the desired look, rather than adjusting the look to fit the elevation" — [Red Blob mapgen4](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/mapgen4/)
- Veloren: `WorldSim::get_map` draws a world map from chunk information — [docs.veloren.net](https://docs.veloren.net/veloren_world/sim/struct.WorldSim.html)
- Dwarf Fortress: advanced world-gen exposes per-map variance/frequency and rejection parameters; seeded so parameter changes give a different world — [DF wiki Advanced World Generation](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Advanced_world_generation)
- Minecraft Wiki's parameter-band tables (continentalness/erosion/PV/temperature/humidity) are themselves a tuning artefact: biome authors place biomes by band index rather than raw values — [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)

### Inferences
- The minimum useful toolkit for the Strata engine: (1) a 2D top-down renderer for each named intermediate (continentalness, erosion, PV, offset, factor, biome ID) over a few km, (2) a spline editor or at least a plotted spline per branch, (3) a "regen radius N" command that reloads parameters from data, (4) a debug density function slot. All of these are cheap if the generator is already a named-node graph.
- Making the generator data-driven (JSON/graph like Minecraft, Hytale, VS, Luanti conf) is what makes the tuning loop fast; hard-coded sums require recompiles.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how long designers spend tuning or gives a documented tuning *process* beyond "iterate with a visualiser"; Kniberg's talk reportedly shows Mojang's internal visualiser but I could not verify its contents.
