# Bake One World Atlas, Fill Chunks Locally

Strata should keep its "chunk = pure function of (key, seed)" contract for everything that touches voxels, and add exactly one global pass per seed: a coarse, cached **world atlas** (eroded heightfield, drainage and lake spill levels, points of interest, road graph, city plan) that every chunk samples read-only. Nothing in the Timberborn, Shadows of Doubt, Lay of the Land or Teardown feature set is achievable without that pass — every shipped system with downhill rivers, road-connected POIs or a coherent city precomputes something per seed, and every system with zero precomputation gives up those features ([Veloren devblog 43](https://veloren.net/devblog-43), [Red Blob Games](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)) — but with it, everything else (terrain shaping, 3D density and caves, scatter, structure stamping, surface rules) remains chunk-local and parallel. On libraries, the decision is narrow: **FastNoiseLite's single C99 header** (MIT, OpenSimplex2/2S, Perlin, Value, Cellular, fBm/ridged, domain warp, 3D rotation) is the only complete noise library callable from a C FFI without a C++ toolchain, with **FastNoise2's C API** as the SIMD upgrade (5–8× faster) once bulk 3D density becomes the bottleneck ([FastNoiseLite](https://github.com/Auburn/FastNoiseLite), [FastNoise2](https://github.com/Auburn/FastNoise2)); nothing permissively licensed exists for roads, parcels, floor plans or furnishing, so those are clean-room ports of well-documented algorithms. The two visual references impose one hard constraint: both **Lay of the Land and Teardown use 10 cm voxels** ([Steam dev reply](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2776090/discussions/0/800091475572429083/), [SED podcast transcript](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/SED1772-Teardown.txt)), which makes a 32³ chunk a 3.2 m cube, forces every generation parameter to be expressed in metres rather than voxels, multiplies voxel count per cubic metre by 1,000, and shifts the source of visual detail from noise to authored assets and surface materials. The recommended order is: noise substrate and determinism; spline-shaped terrain with biomes derived from the same parameters; `.vox` import and template blitting; 3D density with lattice interpolation and ridge-noise caves; the per-seed atlas (stream-power erosion, priority-flood lakes, rivers, water sources); hashed-cell features, structure cells and roads; the city layer; then persistence and editor.

## Ten-centimetre voxels turn a 32³ chunk into a 3.2 m cube

The scale question comes first because it changes every cost figure below. Lay of the Land's developer states that "Each voxel in the game is 10cm. There are 1000 voxels occupying the same volume of space as a single voxels in games that use 1m voxels", and attributes the game's heavy system requirements to "generating and rendering the huge number of voxels" ([Steam discussion, dev reply](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2776090/discussions/0/800091475572429083/)). Teardown's Dennis Gustafsson gives the same figure, "one voxel ≈10 cm, inspired by MagicaVoxel", with each object holding its own dense grid that moves rigidly, and notes that the original OpenGL texture limit capped the world at 4,000 voxels across — 400 m at that scale ([SED1772 transcript](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/SED1772-Teardown.txt)). Press on Lay of the Land describes the result as "pixel-sized miniature cubes" that let round towers, trees and props read as sculpted objects while staying crisply cubic ([Try Hard Guides](https://tryhardguides.com/lay-of-the-land-review/), [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/skyrim-minecraft-lay-of-the-land/)). The look the user wants is therefore not a smoothing technique; it is small cubes under real-time GI. Lay of the Land moved from UE4 to UE5 "mainly to use Lumen" and runs hardware ray tracing by default on recommended hardware ([YouTube devlog y1u9QCRDO5g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1u9QCRDO5g), [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2776090/Lay_of_the_Land/)); Teardown ray-traces against a one-bit-per-voxel occupancy bitmap of the whole level for AO, soft shadows and specular occlusion ([SED1772 transcript](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/SED1772-Teardown.txt), [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/teardown-creator-is-working-on-a-new-voxel-engine)). Strata's Vulkan ray-traced voxel lighting is already the right renderer for this aesthetic; the generation side has to feed it.

Three consequences follow for generation. First, **parameterise in metres**. Minecraft's continentalness noise starts at `base_octave -9`, one period per 512 blocks ([mcmeta continentalness.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/continentalness.json)); Luanti's v7 terrain noise has spread 600 and its roughness selector 2,000 ([minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example)). Those are metre-scale numbers at 1 m voxels; at 10 cm they are 5,120 and 20,000 voxels, and a generator that reads them as voxel counts produces terrain ten times too fine. Every frequency, amplitude, feature radius and cell size in Strata's generator should be a float in metres converted by the voxel scale at the boundary, so the same world definition can be previewed at 1 m and shipped at 10 cm. Second, **budgets multiply by 1,000 per cubic metre but detail does not**. A Timberborn-sized 256×256 m map with 22 m of height is 8×8×1 chunks at 1 m voxels and 80×80×7 = 44,800 chunks at 10 cm. A full-resolution 32³ OpenSimplex2 density at three octaves costs about 2.7 ms scalar per chunk (32,768 samples × 3 × ~27 ns, from the FastNoiseLite benchmark table) ([FastNoiseLite](https://github.com/Auburn/FastNoiseLite)), i.e. two minutes single-threaded for that map; Minecraft-style lattice sampling cuts the noise calls by roughly 81× ([Minecraft Wiki: Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)), and at 10 cm the lattice can be coarser still — an 8- or 16-voxel cell is 0.8–1.6 m, which is the smallest landform anyone needs noise to produce. Lay of the Land's generation is ISPC-compiled SIMD ("2-3x speedup … I can now generate that data fast enough on the fly") and its global creation pass takes "about 2 minutes to 40 seconds" on a Ryzen 7 7800X3D ([X post](https://twitter.com/Tooley1998/status/1795350772693254216), [Steam news feed](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=2776090&count=60&maxlength=0&format=json)) — a direct calibration for what a per-seed atlas plus on-the-fly fill costs at this scale. Third, **sub-metre detail comes from assets and materials, not terrain noise**: trees, boulders, ruins and furniture are authored voxel models stamped into chunks, and surface colour variation comes from per-column material rules. This is exactly Teardown's model, where levels are XML scenes of MagicaVoxel models ([teardowngame.com/modding](https://teardowngame.com/modding/)), and consistent with Lay of the Land's named, repeated structures stamped with "undersides reaching ground" ([Steam news feed](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=2776090&count=60&maxlength=0&format=json)).

One more scale-dependent fact for the Teardown reference: a `.vox` model stores one byte per axis, so a single model is at most 256³ ([ephtracy vox spec](https://github.com/ephtracy/voxel-model/blob/master/MagicaVoxel-file-format-vox.txt)) — 25.6 m at 10 cm. Teardown's guidance is that ~128³ is "generally fine" and hollow objects should be split (trunk/crown, walls/floors) ([teardowngame.com/modding](https://teardowngame.com/modding/)). Any castle or mansion at Lay of the Land's scale is therefore a multi-model scene, so Strata's importer must walk the `nTRN → nGRP → nSHP` scene graph with composed transforms, not just the first `SIZE/XYZI` pair ([vox extension spec](https://github.com/ephtracy/voxel-model/blob/master/MagicaVoxel-file-format-vox-extension.txt)).

## FastNoiseLite's C header replaces hand-written value noise at ~14 ns per 2D sample

Strata's current primitive — 2D value noise with smoothstep, three octaves and a ridged variant — is the cheapest option and also the one with the strongest visible grid bias. Cube-lattice noises (Value, Value-Cubic, Perlin) put features at 45° and 90° to the axes because gradients sit only at grid vertices; Perlin's own course notes call the artefacts "an unavoidable consequence of its underlying algorithm" ([noiseposti.ng](https://noiseposti.ng/posts/2022-01-16-The-Perlin-Problem-Moving-Past-Square-Noise.html), [Perlin, Noise Hardware](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/olano/s2001c24/ch09.html)). For 2D heightmaps the bias is tolerable if octaves are decorrelated (per-octave offsets or seeds, as Red Blob recommends) ([Red Blob Games](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)), but for 3D density it shows up directly as axis-aligned cliffs and overhangs. The fix the sources converge on is OpenSimplex2 with domain rotation ("ImproveXZ" for a Y-up world), which sends one axis up the cube diagonal and comes "with virtually no performance penalty", and OpenSimplex2S where ridged output matters ([noiseposti.ng](https://noiseposti.ng/posts/2022-01-16-The-Perlin-Problem-Moving-Past-Square-Noise.html), [KdotJPG/OpenSimplex2](https://github.com/KdotJPG/OpenSimplex2)). The simplex patent expired on 8 January 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplex_noise)), so there is no licensing reason to stay on value noise.

The cost ladder is known precisely. On an Intel 7820X at 4.9 GHz, FastNoiseLite scalar throughput in Mpts/s is 2D Value 114 / Perlin 93 / OpenSimplex2 71 / Cellular 39 and 3D Value 64 / Perlin 48 / OpenSimplex2 37 / Cellular 12.5 ([FastNoiseLite README](https://github.com/Auburn/FastNoiseLite)); FastNoise2 with AVX2 reaches 3D Value 494 / Perlin 261 / Simplex 268 / Cellular 52 ([FastNoise2 README](https://github.com/Auburn/FastNoise2)). In per-sample terms that is roughly **14 ns for 2D and 27 ns for 3D OpenSimplex2 scalar, 3.7 ns for 3D simplex with AVX2**, and cellular noise at about 5× the cost of value noise in 3D — so a 32×32 heightmap at three octaves is ~43 µs, a full 32³ density is ~2.7 ms scalar or ~0.36 ms SIMD, and cellular should be kept to a single octave. Industry defaults are lacunarity 2.0, gain 0.5, and 3–6 octaves; domain warping costs three fBm evaluations per sample for one warp and five for two, using Quilez's `fbm(p + 4·fbm(p + offsets))` form ([Inigo Quilez, Warping](https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp), [stb_perlin.h](https://github.com/nothings/stb/blob/master/stb_perlin.h)).

Two details of FastNoiseLite matter for a seeded engine. Its lattice hash is `(seed ^ x·501125321 ^ y·1136930381 ^ z·1720413743) · 0x27d4eb2d` ([header](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Auburn/FastNoiseLite/master/C/FastNoiseLite.h)), which XORs the seed in before a single multiply, so seeds that differ in few bits produce related lattices; and v1.1.0 deliberately changed hashing and jitter constants so output differs from v1.0.1 "even with matching seeds" ([releases](https://github.com/Auburn/FastNoiseLite/releases)). Strata should therefore vendor and pin the header, and derive every per-noise, per-octave and per-feature 32-bit seed from the 64-bit world seed through a full-avalanche mixer — PCG's RXS-M-XS hash, which Jarzynski and Olano found "should probably be your default" with better statistical quality than Wang at similar cost, or Eiserloh's SquirrelNoise5 ([Nathan Reed](https://reedbeta.com/blog/hash-functions-for-gpu-rendering/), [JCGT 2020](https://www.jcgt.org/published/0009/03/02/paper.pdf), [SquirrelNoise5 gist](https://gist.github.com/kevinmoran/0198d8e9de0da7057abe8b8b34d50f86)). The same hash is the per-cell RNG for scatter and structures later. Float determinism across platforms requires disabling FMA contraction (`-ffp-contract=off`, `/fp:strict`), avoiding fast-math and libm transcendentals, and testing on at least two architectures ([Box2D determinism](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/determinism/), [Gaffer on Games](https://new.gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/)); Minecraft's `legacy_random_source` flag is the reminder that changing the RNG breaks every existing seed ([minecraft.wiki Custom noise](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Custom_noise)). A golden test — fixed seed, hash of a 32³ density field per architecture — should land with the FFI. Because `float` precision degrades at large coordinates, sample noise in chunk-local coordinates plus an integer chunk origin; at 10 cm voxels a 2 km island is already 20,000 voxels across.

## Splines, not masks, shape terrain; biomes read the same noises

Strata's current shaping — low-frequency fBm plus hill fBm plus a mountain-masked ridged noise summed to a height — is the one-level degenerate case of what Minecraft 1.18 does, and the upgrade is structural rather than a matter of more octaves. Minecraft samples a handful of low-frequency 2D "climate" noises (continentalness at `base_octave -9`, erosion `-9`, weirdness/ridges `-7`, temperature `-10`, vegetation `-8`), domain-warps them through a `shift` noise at `base_octave -3`, folds weirdness into "peaks and valleys" via `PV = 1 − |3·|w| − 2|`, and maps continentalness → erosion → PV through nested cubic splines with explicit derivatives into three scalars: **offset** (surface height), **factor** (vertical squash) and **jaggedness** (peak noise amplitude) ([mcmeta noise files](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/continentalness.json), [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation), [mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)). The spline tables are where the design lives: the ocean branch returns −0.2222 (deep) and −0.12 (shallow) regardless of erosion, the coast band sits at continentalness −0.19…−0.11, a far-inland low-erosion leaf reaches offset 1.5 at PV = 1, a mid-erosion leaf is a flat plateau with a notch to −0.2 at PV = −1 (a river cut), and high erosion collapses everything to −0.02…0.1 lowland ([mcmeta offset.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/offset.json)). Factor runs from about 6.3 (nearly a heightmap) down to 0.6–1.5 where shattered, overhanging terrain is wanted ([mcmeta factor.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/factor.json), [Minecraft Wiki tutorial](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial:Custom_world_generation)). A spline with derivatives expresses plateaus (flat segment), cliffs (steep segment), river cuts (a notch) and coasts (a step) in one table, and nesting by outer coordinate gates whole branches more controllably than multiplying masks.

The biome lesson is the important one for a seam-free chunk-local engine: **terrain parameters first, biomes second, both from the same noises**. Minecraft picks a biome by nearest interval in the 6D (temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion, weirdness, depth) space per 4×4×4 cell, and the biome never dictates height ([Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation), [Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2022/06/05/minecraft-world-generation/)). Because height is a continuous spline of continuous noise, there is no discontinuity to blend; the discrete biome ID only drives surface material and features, which tolerate hard edges. The alternative — per-biome terrain recipes, as in Vintage Story's landforms (a 10-entry octave-amplitude vector plus a Y-key density profile per landform) or Luanti's `height_select` blends — forces a blend of the *parameters* over a kernel or an edge distance (Hytale's `DefaultTransitionDistance` is 32 blocks with a `DistanceToBiomeEdge` node), and community reports show cliffs returning whenever biome selection depends on an axis the terrain ignores ([VS WorldGen Configuration](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Configuration), [Hytale world structure](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/world-structure), [gamedev.stackexchange via mirror](https://backiee.wasmer.app/https_gamedev_stackexchange_com/a/194432)). Luanti's v7 `terrain_persist` noise — a 2 km-scale noise that modulates the persistence of the main fBm between 0.5 and 0.7 — is the cheapest "roughness map" and a direct replacement for Strata's mountain mask ([minetest.conf.example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/minetest.conf.example)). Across engines the scale ladder is consistent: a 1–2 km selector, ~500 m base height, 100–300 m hills and ridges, 10–60 m detail.

Lay of the Land is a bounded island with exactly five biomes (Mystical Forest, Outback Canyons, Mushroom Swamp, Snowy Mountains, Volcano), each with one boss and one cave type, and the developer says new biomes "would be on a separate landmass" ([Fandom wiki: Biomes](https://layoftheland.fandom.com/wiki/Biomes), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2776090/discussions/0/800090026542303360/)). That is a spatial region split of one island rather than a climate lookup, and the chunk-local tool for it is cellular noise in `CellValue` mode (a constant per cell, i.e. a seeded region id) with a domain warp on the input to break the polygonal look, combined with Red Blob's island mask `e = lerp(e, 1 − d, mix)` ([FastNoiseLite wiki](https://github.com/Auburn/FastNoiseLite/wiki/Documentation), [Red Blob Games](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)). For a bounded map, the region split can equally be decided once in the atlas pass, which also lets a designer guarantee "one volcano, at the mountain's centre". Whichever way the biome is chosen, surface material should follow Minecraft's surface-rule model: a per-column decision tree over conditions (`stone_depth`, `noise_threshold`, `steep`, `y_above`, `water`, `vertical_gradient`) that are all pure functions of the column and depth-from-surface counters, with surface depth `floor(surface(x,z)·2.75 + 3 + positional_noise·0.25)` ([Minecraft Wiki: Surface rule](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Surface_rule)). Red Blob's caution that "the value distribution often matters more than the nominal range" and the universal reliance on a 2D visualiser (Misode's generators, Vintage Story's `/wgen regen`, Luanti's Perlin Explorer) argue for making the generator a named-node graph loaded from data from the start, so a top-down renderer of each intermediate and a "regen radius N" command are cheap ([Red Blob Games](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/), [VS WorldGen Configuration](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Configuration)).

## 3D density on a coarse lattice makes caves and overhangs affordable

Every production voxel engine surveyed uses the same recipe for anything beyond a heightmap: `density(x,y,z) = (H(x,z) − y)·squash + A·noise3(x,y,z)`, solid where positive. Notch's 2011 post describes switching from a 2D heightmap ("rather dull", no overhangs) to 3D noise as density, hitting "both performance issues and playability issues", and fixing both by sampling at lower resolution and interpolating: "Suddenly, the game had flat areas, smooth hills, and also most single floating blocks were gone" ([Notch, Terrain generation part 1](https://www.moddb.com/news/terrain-generation-part-1)). Modern Minecraft evaluates its whole terrain-plus-cave tree on 4×8×4 cells with `interpolate_linear`, keeping only the cheap `noodle` term at full resolution, and uses `quarter_negative(...)·4` so the solid side of the gradient is pushed four times harder than the air side — an asymmetry that suppresses floating blobs without flattening cave detail ([mcmeta final_density.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/final_density.json), [mcmeta sloped_cheese.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/sloped_cheese.json)). Luanti's v7 mountains are the same idea with explicit numbers: stone where `np_mountain + gradient ≥ 0`, `gradient = −(y − mount_zero_level)/mount_height`, `np_mountain` spread (250, 350, 250) at 5 octaves and the 3D noise "generated with one extra layer above and below the chunk" ([mapgen_v7.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minetest/minetest/master/src/mapgen/mapgen_v7.cpp)). Hytale's `YSampled` node measured the payoff directly: a TerrainStage at 14 ms without sampling drops to 8 ms at a 4-block sample distance, 6 ms at 8 and 5 ms at 16, with "gains large for 3D noise, small for 2D" ([Hytale density reference](https://hytalemodding.dev/en/docs/official-documentation/worldgen/technical-hytale-generator/density)). For a 32³ chunk, 4×8×4 cells need 9×5×9 = 405 lattice samples instead of 32,768; the one independent benchmark of the interpolation itself warns that a naive trilinear fill can erase the gain (113.8 µs direct vs 114.6 µs naive lerp at only a 2× factor; 89 µs with precomputed t-values) ([saskapult lerp3](https://saskapult.github.io/blog/lerp3)), so the fill loop must be written as flat arrays with precomputed weights. The `squash`-to-amplitude ratio is the single knob for how cavernous the terrain is; the 2D height `H` should keep feeding the density so overhangs cannot detach from the terrain, and a hard constant bottom guarantees a floor ([Roblox devforum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/voxel-generation/338538/12)).

Caves split into two families with different locality properties. **Closed-form ridge caves** are chunk-local by construction: the zero set of one 3D noise is a sheet, so `|n| − t` gives slab-like tunnels (Minecraft's `spaghetti_2d`, confined to an undulating height band by an elevation noise plus a y-gradient), and the intersection of two zero sets is a curve, so `max(|a|, |b|) − t` gives 1–5-block tunnels (Minecraft's `noodle`, with ridge noises at relative frequency 8/3 and thresholds around 0.05–0.1, and the Beta-era `entrances` term that is the only cave allowed to cut the surface band where `sloped_cheese < 1.5625`) ([mcmeta noodle.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/caves/noodle.json), [mcmeta entrances.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/caves/entrances.json), [Minecraft Wiki: Cave](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cave)). Cheese caverns are a low-frequency 3D noise (`cave_cheese` at `base_octave -8` with nine octaves) `min`-ed into the density ([mcmeta cave_cheese.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/noise/cave_cheese.json)); Luanti's equivalent is two 3D noises (`np_cave1/2` at spread 61 and 67, scale 12, 3 octaves) excavated where `contour(c1)·contour(c2) > cave_width` plus a `np_cavern` at spread (384, 128, 384) with a vertical taper ([cavegen.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minetest/minetest/master/src/mapgen/cavegen.cpp)). **Random-walk carvers** — the only technique that produces Minecraft's long rounded tunnels with rooms and branches — are stateful, and Minecraft makes them order-independent by replaying, for each target chunk, the walks of every source chunk in a ±8-chunk square (289 chunks), each reseeded from `((cx·x_mul) + (cz·z_mul)) ^ seed`, with tunnel length tied to the radius (`112 − rand(28)` blocks) and an early-out distance test so most of the 289 candidates die after a few draws ([mc173 cave.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/mc173/latest/source/src/gen/cave.rs)). Luanti instead clamps walks to the chunk plus a halo, which is cheaper but never crosses borders ([cavegen.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minetest/minetest/master/src/mapgen/cavegen.cpp)). Cellular automata need global iteration and a connectivity flood-fill and should be left to bounded dungeon interiors ([Rogue Basin](https://www.roguebasin.com/index.php/Cellular_Automata_Method_for_Generating_Random_Cave-Like_Levels)). For Strata's cubic chunks the pragmatic hybrid is ridge-noise tunnels for the network and a replay-style carver only for rooms, with the walk length capped so a ±2-chunk (5×5×3) neighbourhood suffices. Lay of the Land's biome-specific caves (crystal, sand-pillar, wet) are then the same density terms with per-region parameters and per-region decoration ([YouTube devlog flvqTxpNsVM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=flvqTxpNsVM)).

Two further per-block systems compose with the density for free. Minecraft's 1.18 ore veins are three extra density functions evaluated per block — a `toggle` whose sign picks iron or copper inside Y −60…51, a `ridged` term that gates where the vein exists, and a `gap` term setting a 10–30 % ore-to-filler ratio — and because they share the spaghetti caves' ridge noise, veins hug cave walls ([Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation), [Minecraft Wiki: Ore vein](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Ore_vein)). Surface decoration under 3D terrain uses Luanti's top-down column scan: a "placed since last surface" counter that resets on air or water, so every stone surface under an overhang gets its own top and filler layers, and is set to a sentinel when the block below is air so one-block-thick ceilings stay stone ([mapgen.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minetest/minetest/master/src/mapgen/mapgen.cpp)). Under strict purity the scan needs the density for a few blocks above the chunk (the surface may be in the chunk above), which is cheap on the lattice. An all-air/all-solid early-out follows from bounding the density: fractal amplitude is the sum of octave amplitudes, so a chunk entirely above `max(H) + A/squash` or entirely below `min(H) − A/squash − max cave carve` needs no noise at all, which is what makes streaming on the Y axis affordable.

## Downhill rivers and stable lakes need one global pass per seed

This is the hard dichotomy in the notes, and it decides Strata's architecture. Red Blob states the limitation plainly: "Local calculation means every location is independent of every other location", so pure noise cannot produce "a river flowing from the top of the highest peak down to the ocean" ([Red Blob Games](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/)); Veloren's developers write that "Nobody has ever been able to figure out a way to make river networks that fit the terrain with pure noise" ([Veloren devblog 43](https://veloren.net/devblog-43)). Minecraft's rivers are the PV = −1 valley band where the offset spline notches to −0.2…−0.3 and "river basins are filled with still water up to sea level" — chunk-local, cheap, and level with the sea ([Minecraft Wiki: River](https://minecraft.wiki/w/River), [Minecraft Wiki: World generation](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)). Luanti's `valleys` mapgen had to invent a special reduced-flow liquid because "normal water would easily flow over" its noise-band rivers ([Luanti wiki](https://wiki.luanti.org/Map_generator)). For an engine whose fluid simulation is real and whose target games (Timberborn, Lay of the Land) make water sources and valleys the core mechanic, a river that does not run downhill is a river the fluid sim will drain, and a lake whose rim the terrain does not know about is a lake that empties the moment a player cuts it.

| Approach | Produces | Locality | Cost (published) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noise band (Minecraft PV valleys, Luanti ridges) | Sea-level river channels, no flow | Chunk-local | One extra 2D noise per column |
| Plate-local rivers (Davis 2026) | Downhill rivers inside ~6.7 km plates that never cross plate borders | Region-local, lazy | 3.8 ms per plate (100×100 planning grid), 1.13 µs/sample with rivers vs 0.27 without ([arXiv 2610.00301](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00301v1)) |
| Droplet erosion (Beyer, Lague, McDonald) | Gullies, fans; no coherent network | Halo-local only if droplets are region-seeded; order-dependent | 100k drops ≈ 32–44 s CPU at radius 4; 10k drops 2.3–2.7 s ([Beyer thesis](https://ardordeosis.github.io/implementation-of-a-method-for-hydraulic-erosion/thesis-beyer.pdf), [McDonald](https://nickmcd.me/2020/04/10/simple-particle-based-hydraulic-erosion/)) |
| Pipe-model erosion (Mei, Jákó & Tóth, Šťava) | Water-shaped surfaces at fine resolution | Tiled but lockstep: a global pass | 1024² at 15.5–160 ms/iteration GPU vs 1.6–5.2 s CPU; 2048×1024 at ≥20 fps on a GeForce 8800 ([Jákó & Tóth 2011](https://www.old.cescg.org/CESCG-2011/papers/TUBudapest-Jako-Balazs.pdf), [Šťava 2008](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Stava08SCA.pdf)) |
| Stream-power erosion (Cordonnier 2016, Veloren) | Globally consistent dendritic drainage, lakes, mountains from uplift | Global per seed (receiver tree) | 10k/40k/90k/160k nodes at 0.031/0.177/0.401/1.273 s per step, ~200 steps to converge, ~50 to shape mountains; 1024² grid ≈ 1.25 s/iteration ([Cordonnier 2016](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonier16CGF.pdf), [Lim 2022](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2210.14496)) |
| Priority-flood (Barnes 2014) | Every cell drains; basin spill heights | Global per seed (tileable with a global edge-graph solve) | O(n) integer, O(n log n) float ([arXiv 1511.04463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04463), [Barnes 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/1606.06204)) |
| Thermal erosion / talus | Cliff bands and scree | Chunk-local with a k-cell halo for k iterations | A few stencil passes over 34×34 cells |

Veloren is the closest working precedent to what Strata needs: it treats each map chunk as one node of a Cordonnier-style erosion graph (m = 0.4, n = 1, talus from a rock-strength noise mapped to 15°–60°), runs the whole thing once per seed with "12.5 million years of erosion" so singleplayer startup takes "a minute or two", bakes the altitude map to a `.bin` asset, and then derives river width, depth and velocity per chunk from drainage area via Manning's equation (width ∝ Q^(3/8)) and decides at the filling stage "whether depressions in the terrain should fill with liquid" ([erosion.rs](https://docs.veloren.net/src/veloren_world/sim/erosion.rs.html), [devblog 36 mirror](https://planet.freegamedev.net/?p=149525), [Veloren book](https://book.veloren.net/internals/worldgen/worldgen.html)). Its lake handling solves the exact problem Strata's draining-basin soak test exercises: depression filling each iteration "removes lakes and is slow", so each lake becomes one abstract node, the lowest pass between neighbouring lakes is chosen first, and overflow is routed in O(n + m log m) ([devblog 36 mirror](https://planet.freegamedev.net/?p=149525), [Cordonnier 2016](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonier16CGF.pdf)). Lay of the Land's "rivers carve valleys", "cliff generation", roads that "bridge rivers", and 40 s creation step are consistent with precisely this shape of pipeline — a global heightfield-and-flow pass followed by on-the-fly voxel fill — though the developer has not confirmed the algorithm ([Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2776090/Lay_of_the_Land/), [YouTube devlog y1u9QCRDO5g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1u9QCRDO5g)).

The budget is tractable. Scaling Cordonnier's CPU figures (~8 µs per node per iteration) to a 1024² coarse grid gives about 8 s per iteration single-threaded; 50 iterations is a few minutes single-threaded or around a minute with eight threads, matching Veloren's observed startup. At one atlas sample per chunk column (3.2 m at 10 cm voxels) a 1024² grid covers a 3.3 km island; at one sample per 2 m it covers 2 km. Timberborn-scale maps (official maps run 50×50 to 256×256 cells, with the editor capped at 16 blocks of terrain height and 22 in-game) are trivial: priority-flood on 256² is sub-millisecond-to-millisecond work and directly yields the "every cell drains to an edge" property Timberborn's edge-drain gameplay depends on ([Timberborn wiki.gg: Maps](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Maps), [wiki.gg: Terraforming](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Terraforming)). Timberborn's own authoring primitives are worth copying verbatim into the atlas: a water source is a 1×1×1 emitter with a strength in m³/s (most standard-map sources are strength 1), a negative strength makes it a sink, and map edges drain unless a source sits on them ([wiki.gg: Water Source](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Water_Source)). Both community Timberborn generators are noise-first with rivers as a post-pass, and both warn that regenerating terrain while water exists breaks the simulation ([R-T-B/TimberbornTerrainGenerator](https://github.com/R-T-B/TimberbornTerrainGenerator), [mrCurry90/ExtraTerrainTools](https://github.com/mrCurry90/ExtraTerrainTools)) — Strata should pause and reset its fluid state across regeneration and then run its own fluid sim as the validator, as RTB's README advises.

The division of labour that falls out is: the atlas sets lake levels to **basin spill height − ε** and carves river channels whose width follows drainage; the chunk function samples the atlas bilinearly, adds high-frequency noise whose amplitude is damped near river cells so detail never moves the drainage line by more than the channel half-width, and runs a few thermal-erosion passes on a chunk-plus-halo column heightmap with a noise-driven talus angle to turn ridged spikes into cliffs with scree ([erosion.rs](https://docs.veloren.net/src/veloren_world/sim/erosion.rs.html), [Jákó & Tóth 2011](https://www.old.cescg.org/CESCG-2011/papers/TUBudapest-Jako-Balazs.pdf)); the real-time fluid sim then handles only player-made breaches. Minecraft's aquifer trick — per-cell water levels from noise with a stone barrier forced wherever adjacent cells disagree — is chunk-local and useful for cave pools, but reads as an artificial dam at the surface ([Minecraft Wiki: Aquifer](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Aquifer)). GPU erosion does not change this picture: pipe-model erosion gains 30–100× on GPU but stream-power erosion is a serial tree traversal that converges in seconds-to-minutes on CPU at coarse resolution, so Vulkan compute is worth it only later, as a detail pass adding gullies at block resolution on Šťava-style tiles with copied borders ([Jákó & Tóth 2011](https://www.old.cescg.org/CESCG-2011/papers/TUBudapest-Jako-Balazs.pdf), [Šťava 2008](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Stava08SCA.pdf)).

## Features and structures stay local via hashed cells and padded clipping

Strata's current rule — trees confined to the chunk interior — is the same rule the Pyrite hobby engine uses ("A tree's bounding box cannot exceed the chunk's boundaries") and it produces visible grid artefacts; the deterministic alternative that every shipped engine converges on is a **hashed grid where each cell owns at most one feature** and the cell's global coordinates alone decide existence, species and offset ([Pyrite](https://shivamkr12.github.io/Pyrite/terrain.html), [Frozen Fractal](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2025/11/28/around-the-world-27-planting-trees/)). Frozen Fractal's 1 km chunks do exactly this, with per-biome coverage as a threshold on one simplex layer, per-species slope limits, and a clearing mask around ports — and the post records that hash-based overlap pruning failed because it made results "depend, at least a little bit, on chunk creation order" ([Frozen Fractal](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2025/11/28/around-the-world-27-planting-trees/)). Minecraft's placed-feature vocabulary (`count`, `rarity_filter`, `in_square`, `heightmap`, `noise_based_count`, `noise_threshold_count` sampling `noise(x/200, z/200)`, `biome`, `surface_water_depth_filter`) is the standard density-from-noise idiom to copy, and its own javadoc admits that small salts in `setFeatureSeed` cause correlated placements — one more argument for a strong mixer ([Minecraft Wiki: Placed feature](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Placed_feature), [WorldgenRandom javadoc](https://aldak.netlify.app/javadoc/1.21.1-21.1.x/net/minecraft/world/level/levelgen/worldgenrandom)). Red Blob's jitter study gives the spacing numbers: jitter ≤ 0.6 avoids distance outliers, so 8 m cells give roughly 3–13 m spacing, while true Poisson-disc (Bridson, k = 30, O(N)) is better blue noise but sequential, hence only usable per padded region ([Red Blob jittered grid](https://redblobgames.com/x/1830-jittered-grid), [Bridson 2007](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/bridson-siggraph07-poissondisk.pdf)).

Crossing chunk borders is then a matter of **padded enumeration and clipping**, not neighbour state: a chunk enumerates every cell overlapping its bounds expanded by the maximum feature radius, generates (or looks up) each candidate's voxel set, and writes only the voxels inside itself. godot_voxel documents the pattern — check trees "that would generate in neighbor blocks", intersect their bounds, paste masked so tree air does not overwrite terrain, and pre-generate a library of trees because each is recomputed several times ([voxel-tools: Procedural generation](https://voxel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/procedural_generation/)). Minecraft instead stages generation so that features may write into a 3×3 chunk window and structures are referenced across 17×17, which only works because proto-chunks persist partial state ([neoforged #347](https://github.com/neoforged/Documentation/issues/347), [Minecraft Wiki: Placed feature](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Placed_feature)); deferred overflow buffers are order-dependent unless writes are commutative and should be avoided. With 32-voxel chunks and an 8-voxel radius the padded footprint is 48×48, 2.25× the candidate evaluations; at 10 cm the radius of a 5–7 m canopy is 25–35 voxels, which is why feature bases should be keyed by column cell and each feature's voxels should come from a cached template rather than a per-instance procedure. Luanti's schematic format is the right template record: `size`, per-voxel `{name, prob 0–255, param2, force_place}`, per-slice `yslice_prob`, rotation 0/90/180/270/random and `replacements`, which gives cheap per-instance variety from one asset ([Luanti schematics](https://api.luanti.org/schematics/)). Minecraft's structure template adds alternative palettes and an integrity/decay parameter (max 48³ in Java) ([Minecraft Wiki: Structure file](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Structure_file), [Structure Block](https://wiki.ronlab.site/content/minecraftwiki_en_all_maxi_2025-11/Structure_Block)). Space colonisation gives the most natural crowns but iterates over hundreds to thousands of attraction points, so its place is offline baking of a tree library, not per-chunk work ([Runions 2007](https://www.algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.pdf)).

The `.vox` importer is the bridge to both Teardown and Lay of the Land content. The format is a RIFF-style chunk file: `SIZE` and `XYZI` (one byte per axis, palette index per voxel), `RGBA`, a scene graph of `nTRN` (packed one-byte signed-permutation rotation, integer translation), `nGRP` and `nSHP` nodes, and `MATL` dictionaries with `_diffuse/_metal/_glass/_emit` types ([vox spec](https://github.com/ephtracy/voxel-model/blob/master/MagicaVoxel-file-format-vox.txt), [vox extension spec](https://github.com/ephtracy/voxel-model/blob/master/MagicaVoxel-file-format-vox-extension.txt)). Because rotations are signed axis permutations and translations are integers, blitting an instance into world voxels is exact with no resampling; Teardown's rule that material is set "only by palette index (e.g. index 9 = grass), never by colour" is the simplest mapping onto Strata's block registry — an import table from palette index (or `MATL` type, or `NOTE` names) to block id ([teardowngame.com/modding](https://teardowngame.com/modding/)). `ogt_vox.h` is a single-header MIT reader/writer that resolves instances to column-major 4×4 transforms and is directly linkable through the C FFI ([opengametools](https://github.com/jpaver/opengametools/blob/master/README.md)). Teardown's own procedural surface — Lua "voxscript" with seeded `Randomize`, `CreateBrush` from a `.vox`, `Box/Sphere/Line`, and a PNG `Heightmap` — is a build-time script that writes voxels, and the engine equivalent is a script hook at the template-blit stage ([Teardown voxscript API](https://teardowngame.com/modding/voxscript.html)). Teardown destruction — remove voxels, then split each object's grid into rigid debris by face connectivity, with "hundreds of millions of voxels" per level and no load-path simulation — implies that a Teardown-like game on Strata needs a per-voxel structure id (so a stamped object can be flood-filled and split independently of the terrain grid) on top of the per-voxel edits and fluid wake-ups a Timberborn-like game already needs ([SED1772 transcript](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/SED1772-Teardown.txt)).

Large structures use the same hashed-cell decision at a coarser scale. Minecraft attempts each structure once per `spacing × spacing` chunk cell with a `separation` minimum, a `salt`, a `frequency`, and linear or triangular spread (villages 34/8, mansions 80/20, outposts 32/8 at 20 % with a 10-chunk exclusion from villages) ([Minecraft Wiki: Structure set](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Structure_set)); existence is local per cell, and the piece list — jigsaw expansion from a start pool with weighted pools, fallbacks, a `size` depth of up to 20 and a bounding radius up to 128 blocks — must be deterministic from (seed, cell) and recomputed or memoised by every chunk the box intersects ([Minecraft Wiki: Jigsaw structure](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Jigsaw_structure)). The one part that is not feature-pass-only is terrain adaptation: `beard_thin`, `beard_box`, `bury` and `encapsulate` modify terrain *under* the structure during the terrain stage, which is why Minecraft runs `structure_starts` before `terrain` and why Lay of the Land's "Fixed structure undersides not reaching ground" is a generation-time fix ([Minecraft Wiki: Jigsaw structure](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Jigsaw_structure), [Steam news feed](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=2776090&count=60&maxlength=0&format=json)). Inside a structure box, CGA-style shape grammars (split, repeat, component split on scoped boxes, which in a voxel world are integer AABBs) are deterministic derivations with no search and no contradictions, making them the safer default for buildings; WFC earns its place for irregular ruins and dungeons, solved inside the bounded box with restart-on-contradiction and a template fallback, because open-world WFC is either seamed (free borders), non-deterministic (lazy neighbour constraints) or 4× the work (Boris the Brave's layered modifying-in-blocks, 12 dependency blocks per output block) ([muller_buildings.pdf](https://course.ccs.neu.edu/cs5150f13/readings/muller_buildings.pdf), [Infinite Modifying in Blocks](https://boristhebrave.com/2021/11/08/infinite-modifying-in-blocks), [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)). Roads between POIs — Lay of the Land's defining "hand designed feel" layer, with bridges over rivers and camps along the way — need both endpoints and a path, so they belong in the atlas: Galin's road model minimises a weighted cost of slope, curvature, water and crossing existing buildings with a reuse weight below 1 so later roads share earlier ones, and decides bridge versus tunnel versus surface from the same cost ([Galin 2010 roads](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2010-roads.pdf), [Galin 2012 villages](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2012-villages.pdf)). Minecraft never does this; inter-village roads exist only as mods ([RoadWeaver](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/roadweaver-tec3)).

## A city is a global plan plus greedy room solvers

Shadows of Doubt is less a street-network problem than a building-interior problem. Its hierarchy is City > District > Block > Building > Floor > Address > Room > Tile, with 1.8 m × 1.8 m tiles and 15 × 15-tile floors; mods reveal a small grid of block slots (a 4×5 cap in the editor, 7×8 in the modded Margin City) with the gaps between slots typed as main road, back street or alley, districts and a wealth/land-value field, from which "it still generates the streets, interiors and residents itself" ([ColePowered DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-13-creating-procedural-interiors/), [Margin City](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/The_Protectorate/MarginCity/), [CityPlanner changelog](https://thunderstore.io/c/shadows-of-doubt/p/The_Protectorate/CityPlanner/changelog)). Generation "takes a few minutes", produces a large city file, and the world has 550–730 citizens making 4–10 journeys a day planned in a 10–15 s pre-day pass ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/previews/shadows-of-doubt-preview-city-at-your-fingertips), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/986130/discussions/0/6274121610031111262), [DevBlog 8](https://colepowered.com/?p=34142)). For a grid city the street network is therefore trivial; for anything more organic, the Parish & Müller grower reduces in practice to a priority queue keyed on delay with three snapping events (intersect → crossing, near a crossing → extend to it, near a road → extend to intersect) and pattern rules (New York, Paris radial, San Francisco least-elevation), while tensor-field streamlines (Chen 2008, ProbableTrain's TypeScript port with grid and radial basis fields, RK4 tracing and a per-class `dsep` spacing) give hierarchical grid/radial networks and coast-following streets with blocks found by a right-turn walk ([Parish & Müller 2001](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2001/p_Par01.pdf), [phiresky](https://phiresky.github.io/procedural-cities/presentation.html), [MapGenerator docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ProbableTrain/MapGenerator/master/docs/algorithmoverview.md)). Parcelling on a grid needs only OBB recursive splitting, which a comparison on real blocks found best for gridiron sites, with straight-skeleton subdivision reserved for curved loop and lollipop blocks ([CIRRELT-2015-26](https://www.cirrelt.ca/DocumentsTravail/CIRRELT-2015-26.pdf)). Choosing a city tile equal to one chunk column — as the MIT Lost Cities mod does with one Minecraft chunk per building cell, with per-chunk height sampling and a landscape-correcting pass against adjacent mountains and ocean — makes block layout, chunk generation and LOD coincide ([Lost Cities](https://modrinth.com/mod/the-lost-cities), [changelog](https://modrinth.com/mod/the-lost-cities/changelog)). At 10 cm voxels a 1.8 m tile is 18 voxels and a 15-tile floor is 27 m, so a building footprint spans roughly 8–9 chunk columns; the tile should be chosen so that floor height and wall thickness are integer voxel counts and floors align across adjacent buildings.

For interiors the shipped algorithm is already grid-native and cheap: rooms are placed in importance order (living room, then bathroom, …) by simulating every free placement and scoring it on floor space, shape regularity by corner count, and windows, with connection limits (every room may reach a hallway; kitchens may join living rooms), door limits (bathrooms one door), entrance restrictions, a hallway forced when the entrance is far from a corner, and an override that lets a room take excess area from one already placed ([DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/?p=34642)). The research alternatives are too slow for whole-city work: Merrell's Bayesian-net-plus-Metropolis layouts take "a few seconds to 7 minutes" per house with accessibility failures in 1 of 20 two-storey and 1 of 5 three-storey runs, and Yu's "Make it Home" furnishing takes 22 s for a 20-object living room and up to 376 s for a 64-object shop ([Merrell 2010](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring11/cos598A/pdfs/Merrell10a.pdf), [Yu 2011](https://ww3.math.ucla.edu/camreport/cam11-49.pdf)). Their rules, however, discretise cleanly onto a voxel grid as hard constraints over candidate cells — accessible space in front of an object, a visibility cone, an unblocked BFS pathway between doors, must-touch-wall priors — evaluated SoD-style over every free slot, with anchor objects (bed, sofa, desk) placed before fillers; Yu's user study found that orientation errors are more noticeable than distance errors, so orientation constraints deserve the heavier weight ([Yu 2011](https://ww3.math.ucla.edu/camreport/cam11-49.pdf)). Merrell's door-near-corner rule and regular-interval exterior windows are cheap rules worth copying regardless ([Merrell 2010](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring11/cos598A/pdfs/Merrell10a.pdf)). Multi-storey consistency is best handled the way SoD does it rather than the way Merrell optimises it: a fixed footprint with a shared core column (stairwell, lift shaft, lobby, corridor spine) identical on every floor and addresses filling the remainder, which sidesteps every vertical failure mode Merrell reports. SoD's décor is driven by the assigned citizen — five-colour palettes with Modernity, Cleanness, Loudness and Emotive attributes matched to wealth and personality — which ties furnishing to simulation data without touching geometry ([DevBlog 14](https://colepowered.com/?p=34717)).

The artefact that matters most is not the voxels but the graph. In SoD "the world is divided into rooms connected by doors. Streets are treated as rooms too", doors are the culling portals and carry lock/key/knock/peek semantics, the tile grid drives pathfinding, and the address is the unit of ownership ([DevBlog 22](https://colepowered.itch.io/shadows/devlog/164492/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-22-doors-galore), [DevBlog 13](https://colepowered.com/?p=34642)). Strata's city generator should persist, independent of chunk data: the road graph (nodes, typed edges with width), block polygons with district and wealth, lots with their street-facing edge and door tile, and Building → Floor → Address{type, owner} → Room{type, cells, exterior cells, windows, doors, furniture with blocked and accessible cells} → Door{roomA, roomB, cell, lock state, portal}. That schema is what SoD's mods manipulate and what any NPC-routine or line-of-sight system consumes; for Strata's ray-traced lighting, the door-as-portal relationship is also the natural culling and light-propagation boundary.

## Recommended layered architecture and adoption order

The architecture has five layers, with the global/local boundary drawn exactly once.

| Layer | Runs | Locality | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 Substrate | Everywhere | — | Vendored FastNoiseLite C header (OpenSimplex2 + ImproveXZ, 2S for ridged, Cellular for regions); PCG/Squirrel5 mixer deriving every seed and per-cell RNG from the 64-bit world seed; all parameters in metres; FMA-off build flags; golden-image tests per architecture |
| L1 World atlas | Once per seed at world creation, cached to disk | **Global** | Coarse grid at one sample per 2–3 m: warped continent/island mask and region (biome) ids; stream-power erosion with uplift and talus; priority-flood → spill heights, drainage, receivers; river polylines with width from drainage; lake levels; water sources/sinks in m³/s; POI cells; road graph with bridges; city plan (blocks, districts, lots). Output is small (Veloren stores ~16 bytes per chunk column; Davis's river cache is 0.17 MiB per 10 plates; Genevaux's vector terrain is ~1.5 kB/km²) ([Veloren devblog 32](https://veloren.net/blog/devblog-32), [arXiv 2610.00301](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00301v1), [Genevaux 2013](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf)) |
| L2 Chunk terrain | Per chunk, worker threads | Chunk-local (reads L1) | Bilinear atlas sample + detail noise damped near rivers; spline-shaped offset/factor/jaggedness for unbounded worlds; `(H − y)·squash + noise3` on an 8–16-voxel lattice with precomputed trilinear weights; ridge-noise tunnels and cheese caverns gated below the surface band; per-block ore veins; thermal erosion on a chunk+halo column map; structure-cell lookup for beards/flattening; top-down surface scan with reset-on-air and material rules; all-air/all-solid early-out |
| L3 Features and structures | Per chunk | Chunk-local via padded enumeration | Hashed-cell scatter with per-biome density noise; template library (`.vox`, schematics with `prob`/`yslice_prob`/rotation) blitted with masked paste; structure cells (spacing/separation/salt) with deterministic jigsaw or CGA derivation memoised by cell; WFC only inside bounded boxes; road voxels and bridges rasterised from the L1 graph |
| L4 Authored and persisted | Editor/import/save | Mixed | `.vox` scene import via `ogt_vox.h` with palette→block table and multi-model scene graphs; prefab stamping with terrain adaptation; per-voxel structure id for connectivity-split destruction; chunk save as registry-name palette + bit-packed indices + zstd with a per-chunk data version and fluid state; `.timber`-style single-file map container for small bounded maps with seed, size, sources and start markers as first-class metadata ([Minecraft Wiki: Chunk format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk_format), [luanti world_format.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luanti-org/luanti/master/doc/world_format.md), [fileinfo .TIMBER](https://fileinfo.com/extension/timber)) |

The adoption order below is ordered by dependency and by how much each step unlocks relative to its cost.

**1. Noise substrate, seeding and determinism (days).** Compile `FastNoiseLite.h` with `FNL_IMPL` behind the C FFI, expose 2D/3D sampling and domain warp, replace the hand-written value noise, add the seed mixer, build flags and golden tests, and convert every existing parameter to metres with the voxel scale as a single engine constant. Decide the target scale now: the references argue for 10 cm, and the honest engineering compromise if that proves too heavy is 20–25 cm with the same metre-based definitions, since nothing in the generator should care.

**2. Spline-shaped terrain and derived biomes (a week or two).** Replace the summed fBm with continentalness/erosion/PV noises, warped selectors, nested splines for offset/factor/jaggedness (starting from Minecraft's published control points), a `terrain_persist`-style roughness map, cellular region ids with an island mask for Lay of the Land-style five-region islands, and material rules as a per-column decision tree. Ship the 2D visualiser and a data-driven node graph with it.

**3. `.vox` import and template blitting (days).** This is the cheapest step with the largest visual payoff at 10 cm: a scene-graph-aware importer, a palette-to-block table, a schematic record with per-voxel probability and rotation, and a masked chunk-major blit that dirties each touched chunk once and wakes the fluid sim per chunk. It immediately enables Teardown-style authored scenes and Lay of the Land-style stamped ruins, and it is the template path that step 6 relies on.

**4. 3D density, caves, ores and the surface scan (one to two weeks).** Introduce the density field with lattice interpolation and the Y-axis early-out, ridge-noise tunnels and cheese caverns, ore veins, and the Luanti-style surface scan with a small halo above the chunk. Keep the existing 2D height as the `H` term so overhangs stay attached.

**5. The world atlas (two to four weeks).** Bounded maps first: a priority-flood pass over the coarse grid gives drainage, spill heights and guaranteed edge drainage for Timberborn-style maps in milliseconds; then add Cordonnier/Veloren stream-power erosion with uplift and thermal limits (50 iterations, implicit solver, lake super-graph) for the Lay of the Land look, derive rivers and lake levels, place water sources as m³/s emitters, and make chunks sample the atlas. Validate every generated map by running Strata's own fluid simulation to steady state before accepting it.

**6. Hashed-cell features, structure cells and roads (two to three weeks).** Move trees to hashed cells with padded enumeration and the step-3 template library; add structure cells with spacing/separation/salt, deterministic CGA/jigsaw derivation memoised by cell, and beard-style terrain adaptation evaluated during L2; route roads between POI cells in the atlas with a slope/water/reuse cost and rasterise them with bridges and slope classification (the "cliff generation" layer).

**7. City layer (a month).** Grid-slot city plan in the atlas with districts and land value, OBB parcels, core-column buildings with CGA facades, the SoD greedy room placer with an explicit reachability check, slot-constrained furnishing, and the room/door/address graph as a persisted gameplay artefact.

**8. Persistence and editor (ongoing).** Chunk save format with data versions and fluid state, the single-file bounded-map container, and editor tooling (relative/absolute terrain brushes, source placement with strength, prefab stamping with ghost preview and overwrite policy, a fluid pause/reset/resimulate control, chunk-diff undo).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question from "which generation library" to "where is the one global pass". Noise libraries are a solved, cheap choice — FastNoiseLite now, FastNoise2 when SIMD matters — and the sophistication the target games exhibit comes not from noise but from a seeded, cached atlas that chunks read and never write. That split is also what makes Strata's existing contract an asset rather than a limitation: with the atlas immutable and every chunk-side algorithm (density, ridge caves, hashed scatter, padded template clipping, structure cells, surface rules) a pure function of world coordinates, chunk-border consistency is automatic, all three axes stream freely, and the fluid simulation becomes the validator of generated hydrology rather than its victim.

The 10 cm scale is the decision that quietly determines everything else. It is why both references look sculpted rather than blocky, why their detail is authored (MagicaVoxel assets, stamped structures, per-voxel materials) rather than procedural, why generation must be written in metres, and why the per-chunk budget must be spent on lattice interpolation and template blitting rather than per-voxel noise. A Strata that adopts the atlas and the metre-based definitions can preview a world at 1 m and ship it at 10 cm from the same data, which is the practical path to Lay of the Land's visual sweet spot without committing the engine to a scale before its lighting and fluid costs at that scale are measured.
